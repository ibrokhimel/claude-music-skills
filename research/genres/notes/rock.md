# Rock family: genre recipe cards for FL Studio (rock and roll to modern pop-rock)

Scope: rock and roll, classic rock, hard rock, blues rock, psychedelic rock, punk and pop-punk, post-punk/new wave, alternative/indie, grunge, shoegaze, post-rock, emo/midwest emo, progressive rock, math rock, garage rock revival, modern pop-rock/arena rock. Researched Oct 2026.

Number tags: (sourced) = from the cited page; (convention) = standard practitioner knowledge that I could not tie to a fetched source this session; treat it as a sensible default, not a verified fact; (derived) = computed or inferred from sourced facts.
Source-quality note: most BPM ranges below come from one aggregator ([Orphiq](https://orphiq.com/resources/types-of-rock-music), via search snippet) or are conventions. Wikipedia genre pages describe character and history but almost never give BPMs. Read BPMs as starting points.
Grid notation follows the card format: 16 steps per 4/4 bar (16th notes), `|` between beats. X = accent, x = normal, g = ghost, o = open hat, . = rest. Non-4/4 grids are labeled.

## Q1. What tempo, groove, chord vocabulary, guitar tones and song forms define each genre?

### Takeaway
Rock subgenres differ less in instruments (almost all use guitars, bass, drums and voice) than in five settings: tempo band, straight vs shuffle feel, chord type (open triads vs power chords vs extended/add9 chords vs no chords at all), how distorted and effected the guitar is, and form (3-minute verse-chorus vs 2-minute punk vs 8 to 15 minute builds or suites). Set those five right and the genre reads correctly. The defining harmonic fingerprints are the 12-bar blues (rock and roll, blues rock), the Mixolydian I-bVII-IV (classic rock), minor-pentatonic power-chord riffs (hard rock, grunge), fast I-IV-V / I-V-vi-IV power chords (punk/pop-punk), add9/maj7 open-string voicings in alternate tunings (midwest emo, math rock, shoegaze), and pedal-tone drones (psych, post-rock).

### Cited Findings (recipe cards)

#### Shared rock building blocks (applies to all cards)
- **Backbeat:** rock and roll is a dance rhythm whose backbeat (2 and 4) is "almost always provided by a snare drum" — [Wikipedia: Rock and roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll). Hard rock drums are "driving rhythms, strong bass drum and a backbeat on snare" — [Wikipedia: Hard rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock).
- **Power chord:** root + fifth, often with the octave added (1-5-8). Under distortion the simple 3:2 ratio stays clear, where major/minor thirds turn "messy and indistinct". In drop-D the low three strings let one finger play the shape. Link Wray popularized the distorted power-chord sound in 1958 and the Kinks used fast power-chord changes in 1964 — [Wikipedia: Power chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_chord).
- **Mixolydian vamp I-bVII-IV-I:** the signature classic-rock move. bVII-I resolves without a dominant V. Examples: "Sweet Home Alabama" (D-C-G) — [Benny Sutton](https://bennysutton.com/chords/the-1-flat7-4-1-chord-progression); the "Hey Jude" coda — [Ethan Hein](https://ethanhein.com/wp/2016/musical-simples-hey-jude/).
- **Other borrowed chords (convention):** bVI-bVII-I (in E: C-D-E, the "victory" cadence), i-bVI-bVII (Em-C-D), and bIII as a power chord (E5-G5-A5). Rock rarely uses a functional V7-I except in 12-bar forms.

#### 1. Rock and roll (1950s)
1. **Identity:** USA, late 1940s to early 1950s. Grew out of R&B, gospel, jazz, boogie-woogie and folk. Artists: Chuck Berry, Elvis Presley, Little Richard, Fats Domino, Bill Haley. Landmarks: "Rocket 88" (1951), "Rock Around the Clock" (1954) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll). Also "Johnny B. Goode" and "Tutti Frutti" (convention). By ear: a 12-bar blues at dance tempo with a hard snare backbeat, a boogie bass and a sax or piano lead.
2. **Tempo/feel:** 140–180 BPM for up-tempo numbers (convention). Shuffle (~62–66% swing) or straight 8ths over a swung feel. Rockabilly uses a fast "train beat". 4/4.
3. **Harmony:** "minimal blues chord progressions such as the twelve-bar blues" (sourced, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll)). In A: | A | A | A | A | D | D | A | A | E | D | A | E(turnaround) |. Dominant 7ths are allowed (A7, D7, E7). Harmonic rhythm is 1 chord per bar.
4. **Melody/vocals:** major pentatonic plus blue notes (b3, b7). Shouted, energetic delivery with call-and-response. Chuck Berry double-stop guitar licks (convention).
5. **Instruments:** at first piano or sax led, then electric guitar replaced or joined them in the mid-to-late 1950s. Upright bass, then electric bass (sourced, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll)). DAW: clean/edge-of-breakup tube-amp guitar, upright-bass sample, honky-tonk/upright piano, tenor sax, small kit with slapback delay.
6. **Rhythm:** see the Q2 shuffle grid and rock-and-roll grid.
7. **Bass:** walking boogie line on each chord: 1-3-5-6-b7-6-5-3 in 8ths (convention).
8. **Form:** repeated 12-bar choruses: intro 4 bars, vocal choruses, a sax/guitar solo chorus, then the ending. About 2–3 min (convention).
9. **Mix:** mono-ish, slapback echo (~80–140 ms single repeat) on vocal and guitar, room sound, little compression (convention).
10. **Neighbours:** rockabilly (country + slap bass, train beat), doo-wop, R&B, jump blues.
11. **FL steps:** set swing on the channel rack or use a 12/8 grid. Write the 12-bar in the piano roll. Program the boogie bass. Add a slapback Fruity Delay on guitar/vocal. **Fake-sounding mistakes:** straight quantized 8ths with no swing, distorted modern guitar tone, a huge modern kick.

#### 2. Classic rock (late 1960s–1980s radio canon)
1. **Identity:** a radio/format term more than a style. It covers the 1965–85 British and US mainstream: Rolling Stones, Led Zeppelin, Lynyrd Skynyrd, Tom Petty, Fleetwood Mac, the Eagles (convention). Refs: "Sweet Home Alabama" (I-bVII-IV, [Benny Sutton](https://bennysutton.com/chords/the-1-flat7-4-1-chord-progression)), "Hey Jude" coda ([Ethan Hein](https://ethanhein.com/wp/2016/musical-simples-hey-jude/)). By ear: crunchy (not saturated) guitars on open chords and riffs, a natural-sounding drum kit, and a tight live-band groove.
2. **Tempo/feel:** 90–140 BPM, often around 110–125 (convention). Straight 8ths, sometimes with a light swing.
3. **Harmony:** open-position major chords, I-IV-V, I-bVII-IV, bVI-bVII-I, sus4 moves (Dsus4-D). In D: D-C-G-D. In E: E-D-A-E. In A: A-G-D-A. 1–2 chords per bar.
4. **Melody:** major/minor pentatonic over major chords (the blues-rock "mixed pentatonic"), bends, blues-scale solos (convention).
5. **Instruments:** Les Paul/Strat into a Marshall/Fender amp at crunch, Hammond organ, piano, acoustic guitar doubling, electric bass, and a natural kit with room mics. DAW: amp sim at low-medium gain, B3 organ VST (FL: stock organ or Hammond-type VST), FPC acoustic kit.
6. **Rhythm:** basic rock beat (Q2).
7. **Bass:** root 8ths with passing tones and fills into chord changes.
8. **Form:** verse-chorus-verse-chorus-solo-chorus. 3–5 min (convention).
9. **Mix:** warm tape saturation, moderate compression, guitars panned L/R, organ/keys filling the middle (convention).
10. **Neighbours:** Southern rock (twin harmony leads, Mixolydian), heartland rock, AOR.
11. **FL steps:** write the chord riff first, then double the rhythm guitar (Q4). Use real-sounding room kit samples, and give the guitar mids rather than scooping them. **Fake mistakes:** too much gain, over-quantized drums.

#### 3. Hard rock
1. **Identity:** mid-1960s, from electric blues, psych and garage (Kinks, Who, Cream, Hendrix). Dominant in the 1970s with Led Zeppelin, Deep Purple, Black Sabbath and AC/DC. Glam-metal peak in the 1980s (Mötley Crüe, Bon Jovi, Def Leppard, Guns N' Roses). Pushed aside by grunge/alt in the 1990s. Post-grunge and retro variants since 2000 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock). Refs: "Whole Lotta Love", "Back in Black", "Highway to Hell", "Sweet Child o' Mine" (convention). By ear: riff-driven, distorted guitars with blues bends, a swaggering backbeat, and high, raspy vocals.
2. **Tempo/feel:** 90–140 BPM (convention). Hard rock kept "some swing in the back beat," whereas metal riffs became stand-alone melodies (sourced, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock)). Use about 52–56% swing on 16ths rather than dead straight (derived).
3. **Harmony:** power chords and "repetitive riffs" in E or A minor pentatonic/blues (sourced). Progressions: i-bVII-bVI-bVII (E5-D5-C5-D5); AC/DC open-chord A-D-E with a G or D passing chord; E5-G5-A5 (i-bIII-IV) (convention).
4. **Melody/vocals:** "growling, raspy, or... screaming or wailing", high range or falsetto. Solos use the blues scale with string bends (sourced, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock)).
5. **Instruments:** distorted rhythm and lead guitar, bass "backing for the rhythm and lead guitars", drums (sourced). Marshall-type crunch to high gain. DAW: Plexi/JCM800 amp-sim model, 4x12 cab IR.
6. **Rhythm:** basic rock beat with 8th hats or ride, crash on 1 of sections, tom fills.
7. **Bass:** locks to the kick with 8th-note root pedals and doubles the riff an octave down (convention).
8. **Form:** intro riff 4–8 bars, V 8–16, pre-chorus 4, chorus 8, guitar solo 8–16, chorus x2. 3.5–5 min (convention).
9. **Mix:** big stereo double-tracked rhythm guitars, a dry lead vocal, and a snare with plate reverb. 1980s glam metal adds gated reverb and chorus on clean guitars (convention).
10. **Neighbours:** heavy metal (riff as melody, less swing), glam metal (big choruses, power ballads), post-grunge (drop-D, modern polish), stoner rock (fuzz, slower).
11. **FL steps:** write the riff with a MIDI guitar VST or record DI. Use quad-tracked rhythm (2 L/2 R), a center lead and a bass DI blended with distortion. **Fake mistakes:** MIDI guitar without slides/vibrato, all-downbeat drums with no ghost notes.

#### 4. Blues rock
1. **Identity:** early 1960s, from rock musicians playing Chicago blues. It shaped hard rock, Southern rock and early metal — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues_rock). Artists: Cream, Hendrix, Allman Brothers, ZZ Top, Stevie Ray Vaughan; modern: The Black Keys, Gary Clark Jr., Greta Van Fleet (modern names are convention, not in the fetched page). Refs: "Crossroads" (Cream), "Pride and Joy" (SRV). By ear: 12-bar blues played loud and distorted with long bent-note guitar solos.
2. **Tempo/feel:** 70–140 BPM (convention). Blues rock often uses "straight eighth-note or rock rhythm instead of triplets" (sourced, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues_rock)). Shuffle versions still common (Texas shuffle at 66%).
3. **Harmony:** 12- and 16-bar I-IV-V, mostly major keys, minor sometimes ("Black Magic Woman") (sourced). In E: E7 A7 B7 with quick-change IV in bar 2 (convention).
4. **Melody:** minor pentatonic + b5 blue note over dominant chords, quarter-tone bends on b3, wide vibrato, call-and-response between vocal and guitar (convention). "String-bending blues-scale" solos and "extended boogie jams" (sourced).
5. **Instruments:** guitar, bass, drums, sometimes keys and harmonica (sourced). DAW: Tube Screamer into Fender/Marshall amp sim, Hammond, harmonica sample.
6. **Rhythm:** shuffle grid (Q2) or straight boogie.
7. **Bass:** boogie/walking 1-3-5-6-b7 or riff doubling.
8. **Form:** 12-bar choruses: head x2, solo choruses x2–4, head out. 4–8 min live (convention).
9. **Mix:** live-room feel, less double-tracking, amp breakup from volume (convention).
10. **Neighbours:** Southern rock, boogie rock (ZZ Top "La Grange" style shuffle-boogie), garage revival duos (Black Keys).
11. **FL steps:** program the bass and drums first with 66% swing. Write the guitar solo with pitch-bend automation on bends (a full or half step up, held). **Fake mistakes:** solo notes quantized to the grid, no bends or vibrato, straight hats under a triplet bass.

#### 5. Psychedelic rock
1. **Identity:** 1965–66 onwards. The 13th Floor Elevators used "psychedelic rock" on business cards in January 1966. "Norwegian Wood" (Rubber Soul, Dec 1965) brought sitar into rock. Peak 1967–68: Sgt. Pepper's (May 1967), Jefferson Airplane's Surrealistic Pillow (Feb 1967) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_rock). Modern neo-psych: Tame Impala, King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard, Khruangbin (convention). Refs: "Tomorrow Never Knows", "White Rabbit", "Purple Haze"; modern "Let It Happen" (convention). By ear: drones, modal melodies, swirling phased/flanged sounds, backwards sounds and long jams.
2. **Tempo/feel:** 70–130 BPM (convention). Often mid-tempo, sometimes with odd-meter or tempo shifts. "Occasional key and time signature changes" (sourced).
3. **Harmony:** "modal melodies and drones" with Indian influence (sourced). Mixolydian or Dorian vamps over a pedal tone. In C Mixolydian: C-Bb-F over a C drone. Dorian vamp in A: Am7-D (i-IV). Chromatic descending lines (Am-Am/G#-Am/G-D/F#) (convention).
4. **Melody/vocals:** modal and melismatic, Indian-style slides, double-tracked or ADT vocals, dreamy delivery (convention).
5. **Instruments:** guitars with "feedback, wah-wah and fuzzbox"; "backwards tapes, panning, flanging, tape loops, and extreme reverb"; sitar, tambura, tabla; organ, harpsichord, Mellotron (sourced, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_rock)). DAW: fuzz pedal sim, Fruity Flanger/Phaser, reversed reverb tails, a Mellotron flute/strings sample, organ, sitar sample. Modern neo-psych: heavily compressed (pumping) drums, phaser on everything, synth bass.
6. **Rhythm:** basic rock beat, often tom-heavy (Ringo-style), plus a tambourine 8th/16th layer.
7. **Bass:** melodic, McCartney-style counter-lines, or a drone pedal (convention).
8. **Form:** "extended instrumental segments... jams" and "disjunctive song structures" (sourced). Pop psych is 3 min; jam psych is 6–20 min.
9. **Mix:** hard-panned 1960s stereo (drums one side), auto-panning, tape flanging. Modern: side-chained/pumping drum bus, lo-fi saturation (convention).
10. **Neighbours:** acid rock (heavier, jam), space rock (long delays, synth), krautrock (motorik), stoner rock, neo-psychedelia.
11. **FL steps:** start with a 1–2 chord vamp and a drone. Add Gross Beat or a reversed sample for backwards swells. Automate phaser and flanger rates. Put a tambourine on 16ths. **Fake mistakes:** busy chord changes (psych uses few), clean digital reverb instead of tape and spring textures.

#### 6. Punk rock
1. **Identity:** NYC (CBGB) and London, 1974–77: Ramones, Sex Pistols, the Clash, Television, Patti Smith — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock). Refs: "Blitzkrieg Bop", "Anarchy in the U.K.", "London Calling" (convention). By ear: fast, short, loud, buzzsaw guitar chords, and a shouted, sneering vocal.
2. **Tempo/feel:** "short" songs at "fast tempos" (sourced, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock)). Classic punk 160–200 BPM, hardcore 180–220+ (sourced, [Orphiq via search](https://orphiq.com/resources/types-of-rock-music), aggregator). Straight 8ths with no swing. Most early punk kept "verse-chorus form and 4/4" and "syncopation is much less common" (sourced).
3. **Harmony:** "highly distorted power chords or barre chords" in a "buzzsaw drone" (sourced). Three-chord I-IV-V: in E, E5-A5-B5. I-bVII-IV: E5-D5-A5. I-bIII-IV: E5-G5-A5. All-downstroke 8th-note picking (convention). 1–2 chords per bar.
4. **Vocals:** "sometimes nasal", "shouted in an arrogant snarl". Guitar solos avoided as "self-indulgent", but basic breaks were common (sourced).
5. **Instruments:** "one or two guitars, bass, drums and vocals"; drums "heavy and dry" (sourced). DAW: one mid-gain (not metal) amp sim, little reverb.
6. **Rhythm:** punk 8th beat, skank/polka beat, d-beat (Q2).
7. **Bass:** "uncomplicated", "relentless, repetitive" 8th-note roots (sourced). Dee Dee Ramone-style downpicked root 8ths that follow the guitar (convention).
8. **Form:** V-C-V-C-(short break)-C. Under 2.5 min. Intro is often a count-in or 4-bar riff (convention).
9. **Mix:** "minimalistic", sometimes recorded on home four-tracks (sourced). Dry and mid-forward, with limited stereo tricks.
10. **Neighbours:** hardcore (faster, shouted, d-beat/skank), skate punk (melodic + fast), crust/d-beat, oi!, pop-punk (below).
11. **FL steps:** tempo 170–190. Program kick/snare 8th grid, power chords in the piano roll on a guitar VST with 8th downstrokes (all-down velocity ~100–115). Double the guitar hard L/R. **Fake mistakes:** too-heavy modern metal gain, quantized-perfect drums at tempo, long songs.

#### 7. Pop-punk
1. **Identity:** pioneers Buzzcocks, Ramones and Undertones. The Descendents' 1982 album set the US template. Mainstream from 1994 with Green Day's Dookie and the Offspring's Smash. Second wave 1997–2004: blink-182's Enema of the State (1999), Sum 41, New Found Glory, Avril Lavigne's Let Go (2002). Emo-pop 2004–10: Fall Out Boy, My Chemical Romance, Paramore. Revival from 2020: Machine Gun Kelly's Tickets to My Downfall (2020, #1 Billboard 200), Olivia Rodrigo's "Good 4 U" (#1, 2021) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop-punk). By ear: punk speed and power chords under sweet, pop-melodic vocal hooks and big gang choruses.
2. **Tempo/feel:** 150–190 BPM (sourced, [Orphiq](https://orphiq.com/resources/types-of-rock-music), aggregator). "Fast-paced, energetic tempos" (sourced, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop-punk)). Uses half-time pre-choruses or bridges (convention).
3. **Harmony:** power chords in pop progressions: I-V-vi-IV (in E: E5-B5-C#5-A5), vi-IV-I-V, I-IV-vi-V. Palm-muted 8ths in verses, open ringing chords in choruses (convention). "Sweet harmonies against bratty, rowdy riffs" (sourced).
4. **Vocals:** clean, nasal, adolescent, with doubled lead vocals and gang "whoa-oh" backing. Melody is major-key, stepwise, syllabic (convention).
5. **Instruments:** 2 guitars, bass, drums. Modern revival adds 808s/trap hats and programmed elements (MGK and Travis Barker era) (convention).
6. **Rhythm:** verse 8th beat with kick on "&" of 2; chorus skank beat on an open ride or crash. Travis Barker-style fast hat/snare fills (convention). Grids in Q2.
7. **Bass:** 8th roots, with melodic walk-ups into choruses (convention).
8. **Form:** Intro 4–8, V1 8–16, pre 4–8 (half-time), C 8, V2, pre, C, bridge/breakdown 8 (half-time, palm-mute build), C x2. 2.5–3.5 min (convention).
9. **Mix:** polished, very tight, heavily edited drums, quad-tracked guitars and a bright, loud master. "From raw... to polished and suitable for mainstream radio" (sourced).
10. **Neighbours:** skate punk (faster, more technical), emo-pop (darker, more dramatic), easycore (with metalcore breakdowns).
11. **FL steps:** 170 BPM. Build the drum pattern by section (8th beat > skank chorus > half-time bridge). Use palm-muted MIDI guitar with a palm-mute keyswitch in verses and open in choruses. Stack gang vocals as 4–8 layered takes. **Fake mistakes:** no palm-mute/open dynamic contrast, no crash on chorus downbeats, sloppy timing (the genre is tight).

#### 8. Post-punk and new wave
1. **Identity:** UK, late 1970s, as a reaction to punk's commercialization. Reynolds dates the era about 1978–84. UK: Siouxsie and the Banshees, PiL, Joy Division, Gang of Four, Wire, Magazine. US: Talking Heads, Pere Ubu, Devo, no wave. Australia: Birthday Party, Go-Betweens. Key LPs: Metal Box, Unknown Pleasures, Entertainment! — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-punk). New wave was the more commercial, pop-leaning branch (sourced). There was a 2000s NYC revival (dance-punk). 2010s–20s UK/Irish wave: Fontaines D.C., IDLES, Shame, Squid, Black Country New Road (named in the article's contents but not detailed; convention). Refs: "Love Will Tear Us Apart", "Damaged Goods", "Once in a Lifetime"; new wave "Just What I Needed", "Rio" (convention). By ear: a melodic bass carries the tune, with thin, angular or chorused guitars, mechanical drums and deadpan or spoken vocals.
2. **Tempo/feel:** 110–150 BPM (sourced, [Orphiq](https://orphiq.com/resources/types-of-rock-music), aggregator). "Steady, often mechanical drumming (including drum machines)" (sourced). Straight, or disco/funk 16ths in dance-punk.
3. **Harmony:** abandoned "three-chord progressions and Chuck Berry-based guitar riffs" (sourced). Minor keys, two-chord vamps, single-note guitar lines, dissonant intervals (b2, tritone), and the bass playing the melody while guitar plays drone or texture. In D minor: Dm-Bb (i-bVI), or Am-F-C-G with the bass in the high register (convention). New wave: bright synth-pop I-V-vi-IV and major 7ths.
4. **Vocals:** "spoken singing", unconventional, from baritone deadpan (Ian Curtis) to yelped (Byrne) (sourced/convention).
5. **Instruments:** "melodic basslines" freed guitarists to experiment. Influences from funk, disco, dub/reggae, electronics, jazz and the avant-garde (sourced). DAW: bass with chorus and pick attack (play it an octave up), guitar with chorus/flanger or clean treble, a drum machine or gated toms, analog synth pads (FL: 3x Osc/Sytrus/Harmor for new wave synths).
6. **Rhythm:** tom-heavy tribal grooves (Joy Division/Banshees); dance-punk four-on-floor with disco open hats (Q2).
7. **Bass:** the lead instrument: repeating 1–2 bar 8th-note melodies high on the neck, with a pick (convention, consistent with sourced "melodic role").
8. **Form:** verse-chorus but repetitive and vamp-based. 3–5 min (convention).
9. **Mix:** dub-derived space: long delays, gated/dry contrast, cold reverb (Martin Hannett-style), stark separation (sourced "dub and reggae production methods"; Hannett detail is convention).
10. **Neighbours:** gothic rock (slower, darker, flanged), dance-punk (disco beat), new wave (pop synths), coldwave/darkwave (synth-led), 2020s "talk-rock"/Speak-sing post-punk.
11. **FL steps:** write the bass hook first. Drums: Fruity Drum machine-style samples or a gated tom kit. Guitar: chorus + slapback delay, single notes. Vocals: dry-ish talk-sung. **Fake mistakes:** a big root-note bass with wide distorted power-chord guitars (that is grunge/alt, not post-punk).

#### 9. Alternative rock and indie rock
1. **Identity:** from the late-1970s underground; established in the 1980s via college radio: R.E.M., the Dream Syndicate, Sonic Youth, Hüsker Dü, the Pixies. Mainstream in 1991 with Nirvana's Nevermind. Britpop: Oasis, Blur. After Nirvana, "indie rock" meant artists who would not or could not cross over (Matador, Dischord). By the 2010s "indie" largely replaced "alternative" in US discourse. Twenty One Pilots and Muse fuse rock with electronica and hip-hop — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock). Refs: "The One I Love", "Where Is My Mind?", "Seven Nation Army", "Mr. Brightside" (convention). By ear: guitars that "blast out power chords, pick out chiming riffs, buzz with fuzztone and squeal in feedback" (sourced), quiet-verse/loud-chorus dynamics and introspective vocals.
2. **Tempo/feel:** indie 110–140 BPM; rock in general 100–160; indie 90–160 (sourced, [Orphiq](https://orphiq.com/resources/types-of-rock-music), aggregator; the two indie ranges conflict, so read 90–160 as the full span and 110–140 as typical). Straight 8ths.
3. **Harmony:** jangle open chords with add9/sus2 (Cadd9, Dsus2, Em7 shapes keeping the top strings ringing); I-iii-IV, vi-IV-I-V; Pixies-style chromatic power chords; Britpop I-V-vi-IV with open chords (convention).
4. **Vocals:** "emotionally introspective" (sourced). Unpolished, conversational, double-tracked choruses.
5. **Instruments:** Rickenbacker-style jangle (R.E.M.) or Jazzmaster/Jaguar fuzz. DAW: clean amp sim with compressor and chorus for jangle, Big Muff-style fuzz for choruses.
6. **Rhythm:** basic rock beat. "Indie disco" four-on-floor with offbeat hats (2000s).
7. **Bass:** root 8ths that become melodic in verses (Kim Deal-style simple hook lines) (convention).
8. **Form:** V-C-V-C-bridge-C with a loud-quiet switch. 3–4 min.
9. **Mix:** less compressed and more roomy than pop-rock. Lo-fi or tape textures accepted in indie.
10. **Neighbours:** college rock, Britpop, jangle pop, noise rock (Sonic Youth: detuned, feedback), indie folk, bedroom pop.
11. **FL steps:** two guitars, one clean jangle and one fuzz entering at the chorus. Automate the drum level up 2–3 dB in choruses. **Fake mistakes:** pop-punk-tight editing, over-polished vocals.

#### 10. Grunge
1. **Identity:** Seattle/Olympia, mid-1980s, fusing punk and heavy metal with influences from Sonic Youth, Pixies and Dinosaur Jr. Sub Pop, Green River, Melvins, Mudhoney. Defining LPs: Nevermind (1991), Ten, Badmotorfinger, Dirt — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grunge). Refs: "Smells Like Teen Spirit", "Black Hole Sun", "Would?", "Alive" (convention). By ear: sludgy, mid-heavy downtuned distortion, quiet clean verses exploding into loud choruses, and raspy, slurred vocals.
2. **Tempo/feel:** "slowed-down punk", "slow, sludgy" (sourced, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grunge)). Classic grunge 100–140 BPM, sludge 80–110 (sourced, [Orphiq](https://orphiq.com/resources/types-of-rock-music), aggregator). "Teen Spirit" is about 117 BPM (convention).
3. **Harmony:** "downtuned" guitars (drop D, Eb standard, drop C#/C for Alice in Chains/Soundgarden — convention), "dissonant harmonies" (sourced). Power chords moving in 4ths and chromatics with bVI/bIII: in F minor, F5-Bb5-Ab5-Db5 (i-iv-bIII-bVI, the "Teen Spirit" shape, convention). Drop-D riff: D5 pedal with chromatic F5-E5-Eb5 moves. Soundgarden-style odd meters (7/4 in "Spoonman" and "Outshined" sections, convention).
4. **Vocals:** "gruff, slurred", "gravelly, raspy", with "growls, moans, screams and mumbles" (sourced). Layne Staley-style stacked harmonies a 3rd or 4th apart (convention).
5. **Instruments:** "sludgy... thick middle register and rolled-off treble", high distortion. Boss DS-1/DS-2, Big Muff, Small Clone chorus on clean verses, Marshall amps, Fender Jaguar/Mustang offsets (sourced). DAW: DS-1 or Muff sim into a Marshall model, LPF around 6–8 kHz to roll off treble (derived from sourced "rolled-off treble"), chorus on clean verse guitar.
6. **Rhythm:** big, slightly behind-the-beat rock beat with washy crash-ride. Verse is sparse kick/snare; chorus opens up (Q2 grid).
7. **Bass:** "ponderous basslines" (sourced): roots, often overdriven, doubling the riff.
8. **Form:** "stop-start" and alternating soft and loud sections (sourced). Intro riff 4–8, quiet V 8–16, loud C 8, V, C, solo (often melodic, mirroring the vocal) 8, C x2. 3.5–5 min.
9. **Mix:** big drum room sound (Butch Vig/Steve Albini), guitars thick in the mids, less glossy than 1980s rock (convention).
10. **Neighbours:** post-grunge (Foo Fighters, Creed, Nickelback: polished, radio-friendly), sludge (slower, heavier), alt-metal, 2020s "grunge-gaze" revival (convention).
11. **FL steps:** drop-D power chords in the piano roll (D2-A2-D3). A clean chorused verse guitar mono-center, then a doubled fuzz chorus hard L/R. Automate the drums from closed hat to crash-ride. **Fake mistakes:** a bright, scooped metal tone, verse and chorus at the same loudness.

#### 11. Shoegaze (and 2020s nu-gaze)
1. **Identity:** Britain (London/Thames Valley), late 1980s to early 1990s. The term was coined in 1991; first print use is NME, 25 May 1991, describing Slowdive as "shoe-gazers". Kevin Shields' "glide guitar" appeared on MBV's 1988 records, and the peak was Loveless (Nov 1991). Ride's Nowhere (1990), Slowdive's Souvlaki (1993), Lush, Chapterhouse, Swervedriver — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoegaze). A Gen Z/TikTok "zoomergaze" revival followed (sourced heading, same page). Stereogum frames 2023 as the start of a new shoegaze era. In 2025 MBV returned to play its biggest non-festival shows and the Slide Away festival sold out multi-thousand venues ([Stereogum 2025 list, via search snippet](https://www.stereogum.com/2482752/the-10-best-shoegaze-albums-of-2025/lists/year-in-review/2025-in-review)). New bands mix the noise wall with emo/post-hardcore hooks (same snippet). Refs: "Only Shallow", "When the Sun Hits", "Vapour Trail", "Alison" (convention). By ear: a wall of fuzzy, reverbed, pitch-wobbling guitars where "no instrument is distinguishable", with vocals buried in the mix.
2. **Tempo/feel:** no sourced BPM range. 70–130 BPM, often mid-tempo dreamy (convention). Straight 8ths, sometimes half-time. Loveless-era MBV tracks often use driving 8ths at 110–130 (convention).
3. **Harmony:** major-leaning chords with open strings, add9/maj7, alternate/open tunings; slow harmonic rhythm (1 chord per 1–2 bars) (convention). In E: E-Emaj7/G#-A(add9)-A (I-I-IV-IV with an open B/E drone). Glide guitar bends whole chords with the tremolo arm while strumming, so the harmony wobbles in pitch (sourced technique; exact description is convention).
4. **Vocals:** "ethereal", "obscured vocals", mixed low (sourced). Breathy, doubled, often male/female unison.
5. **Instruments:** reverb, delay, chorus, tremolo, distortion; "bent or flanged guitars"; a Spector-like wall of sound (sourced). Reverse reverb and "soft focus" reverb with parallel delays (e.g. 250 ms and 380 ms in the Keeley Loomer design) are signature pedal textures ([Keeley Loomer listing](https://reverb.com/item/3489147-keeley-loomer-fuzz-reverb-cme-exclusive)). DAW: Big Muff/fuzz sim, reverse reverb (FL Fruity Reeverb 2 or Valhalla), long shimmer reverb at 100% wet on a send, plus pitch-wobble automation (pitch bend ±20–50 cents or a vibrato/chorus) (convention).
6. **Rhythm:** simple, steady backbeat with washy crash-ride or a driving 8th snare/tom feel. Drums are relatively clear while guitars blur (Q2).
7. **Bass:** root notes holding the harmony, as an anchor under the blur.
8. **Form:** verse-chorus but blurred. Long instrumental noise sections, outro noise wash. 4–6 min (convention).
9. **Mix:** guitars loudest, vocals tucked in as another texture, everything soaked in reverb, a wide wall. Modern nu-gaze adds digital clipping, Auto-Tune and drum machines ([Paste: hyper-rock](https://www.pastemagazine.com/music/scene-report/the-emergence-of-hyper-rock)).
10. **Neighbours:** dream pop (cleaner, less distortion), blackgaze (black-metal blast beats + gaze), nu-gaze/hyper-rock (Auto-Tuned, glitchy, bedroom: Parannoul, Jane Remover, Alex G, Spirit of the Beehive, feeble little horse, yeule — [Paste](https://www.pastemagazine.com/music/scene-report/the-emergence-of-hyper-rock)), slowcore.
11. **FL steps:** 3–6 guitar layers (fuzz chords L/R, an e-bow/feedback drone, a reverse-reverb swell). Bus everything into a long reverb and glue compressor. Automate pitch wobble. Tuck the vocal 3–6 dB under the guitars. **Fake mistakes:** a clean, upfront vocal; a dry guitar; a tight metal tone; too many chord changes.

#### 12. Post-rock
1. **Identity:** Simon Reynolds coined the term (late 1993, defined 1994) as "using rock instrumentation for non-rock purposes". First wave: Slint's Spiderland (1991), Talk Talk's Spirit of Eden and Laughing Stock, Tortoise's Millions Now Living Will Never Die (1996). Second wave: Godspeed You! Black Emperor, Sigur Rós, Mogwai, Explosions in the Sky, This Will Destroy You — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-rock). Refs: "Your Hand in Mine" (EITS), "Mogwai Fear Satan", "Hoppípolla" (convention). By ear: long instrumental pieces that build slowly from quiet clean guitar loops to a huge distorted climax.
2. **Tempo/feel:** "irregular tempos" (sourced). Typically 60–140 BPM, often slow, with a half-time or double-time switch at the climax (convention).
3. **Harmony:** guitars used as "noise generators" or a "palette of textures"; "alternate tunings... delay and distortion, EBows, and looping" (sourced). Diatonic major/minor, often a 2–4 chord loop such as vi-IV-I-V (in D: Bm-G-D-A) over a pedal, tremolo-picked melodies on top. Harmonic rhythm 1–2 bars per chord (convention).
4. **Melody:** a repeated tremolo-picked or delay-arpeggiated melody that grows by layering octaves (convention). Usually instrumental (sourced).
5. **Instruments:** standard rock kit used unconventionally. Bass as "sustained drones, pulsating loops". Drums with "motorik" influence, "multiple drum kits... minimalist patterns" (sourced). Often glockenspiel, strings, piano. DAW: delay (dotted-8th) on clean guitar, e-bow sound (sustained guitar or bowed pad), strings, glockenspiel.
6. **Rhythm:** crescendo map (Q2).
7. **Bass:** drones or pedal roots that move only at section changes.
8. **Form:** "repetitive build-ups of timbres, dynamics and textures... extremely wide range of dynamics", "climactic endings" (sourced). 6–15 min, often 8–10 (convention). Typical map: ambient intro 16–32 bars, theme A clean 16–32, build 16–32, climax 16–32, decay/outro 8–16 (convention).
9. **Mix:** wide dynamic range (less limiting), lots of reverb, cinematic (convention).
10. **Neighbours:** math rock (more rhythmically complex), slowcore, ambient, cinematic (see earlier cinematic notes), krautrock, blackgaze.
11. **FL steps:** build a 4-bar loop, then arrange 5 sections by adding a layer every 8 bars. Automate velocity and volume up across the build, then add a reverb throw and silence before the climax. **Fake mistakes:** reaching full loudness too early, a mastering limiter that crushes the dynamic arc, vocals.

#### 13. Emo and midwest emo
1. **Identity:** DC hardcore, 1985 (Rites of Spring, Embrace): hardcore structures with "melodic guitars, varied rhythms, and personal, emotional lyrics" — [Wikipedia: Emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emo). Midwest emo in the 1990s: Gauge, Braid, Cap'n Jazz, The Promise Ring, American Football, The Get Up Kids. Fourth wave (2007–19): Tigers Jaw, Algernon Cadwallader, Snowing, Modern Baseball. Fifth wave from 2020, which became a TikTok meme — [Wikipedia: Midwest emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest_emo). 2000s mainstream emo merged with metalcore and pop-punk (screamo, breakdowns) ([Emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emo)). Refs: "Never Meant" (American Football), "The Middle" (Jimmy Eat World), "Welcome to the Black Parade" (convention). By ear (midwest): twinkly clean arpeggios in open tunings, odd phrase lengths, and strained, untrained, heart-on-sleeve vocals.
2. **Tempo/feel:** 90–170 BPM. Midwest emo often 120–160, with frequent meter shifts (3/4, 6/8, 7/8 bars) (convention). Mainstream emo matches pop-punk tempos.
3. **Harmony:** "cycling guitar parts", "arpeggiated melodies", "twinkly" guitar, math-rock techniques, "gloomy chord progressions" (sourced, [Midwest emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest_emo)). Open tunings (American Football: FACGCE, convention), maj7/add9/sus2, chords built on open-string drones. Example in F: Fmaj7-Am7-Bbmaj7(#11)-C(add9) (I-iii-IV-V with extensions) (convention). Tapped interval figures.
4. **Vocals:** 1990s "breathy head voice, often straining at the top of their range" ([Emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emo)). "Unconventional singing" with "no vocal training but plenty of heart" ([Midwest emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest_emo)). Gang shouts at climaxes. Occasional trumpet (American Football).
5. **Instruments:** "clean guitar timbres and bursts of distortion" ([Emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emo)). Clean Telecaster/Jazzmaster into a Fender-style clean amp with light compression. "Chugging bass lines" (sourced, [Midwest emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest_emo)).
6. **Rhythm:** busy, fill-heavy drumming with hat-snare interplay and odd-bar phrasing. Half-time climaxes.
7. **Bass:** melodic counterpoint, 8th chugs in loud parts.
8. **Form:** "unorthodox song structures, and extreme dynamic shifts" (sourced, [Emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emo)). Through-composed, ending in a loud, cathartic final section. 3–5 min.
9. **Mix:** natural, roomy, mid-fi; modern revival is somewhat lo-fi (convention).
10. **Neighbours:** math rock (more technical, often instrumental), screamo (screamed, chaotic), emo-pop (polished pop-punk), post-emo, emo rap (not rock: trap beats with emo guitar loops; see earlier hip-hop notes).
11. **FL steps:** write 2 interlocking clean guitar arpeggio lines in an open tuning (16ths, let notes ring with long note-offs). Change meter for single bars. Build to a distorted, shouted finale. **Fake mistakes:** a polished pop vocal, everything in 4/4 square 8-bar phrases, distorted verse guitars.

#### 14. Progressive rock
1. **Identity:** US/UK, mid-to-late 1960s; peak 1971–76: Pink Floyd, Yes, King Crimson, Genesis, Emerson Lake & Palmer. Progressive metal matured with Dream Theater's Images and Words (1992) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_rock). Modern: Tool, Porcupine Tree, Steven Wilson, Opeth, Haken, Polyphia-adjacent prog (convention). Refs: "Roundabout", "21st Century Schizoid Man", "Firth of Fifth", "Money" (7/4) (convention). By ear: long multi-section pieces, odd meters, virtuosic unison runs, and Mellotron/Hammond/Moog keyboards.
2. **Tempo/feel:** varies widely within a piece. Meters 7/8, 5/4, 7/4, 9/8, 11/8 and frequent changes (convention; Pink Floyd "Money" is 7/4, Genesis "Apocalypse in 9/8").
3. **Harmony:** "complex chord changes", "harmonic language imported from jazz and 19th-century classical" (sourced). Modal (Lydian, Dorian), slash chords, modulations, ostinatos over shifting chords. Example in E: Emaj7 - F#/E - G#m7 - Asus2 (Lydian colour via F#/E) (convention).
4. **Melody:** themes that return in leitmotif fashion, keyboard and guitar unison lines, fantasy or concept vocals (convention).
5. **Instruments:** Hammond organ, Minimoog, Mellotron (sourced); Rickenbacker bass, big kits with gongs and tuned percussion. DAW: Mellotron sample, Moog-type mono lead (FL: Sytrus/3x Osc/Minimoog-style VST), B3 organ with rotary.
6. **Rhythm:** odd-meter grids (see math rock in Q2).
7. **Bass:** melodic and virtuosic (Chris Squire), often doubling keyboard lines.
8. **Form:** "long solos, extended pieces", "song cycles and suites made up of multiple movements" (sourced). 6–25 min, concept albums.
9. **Mix:** hi-fi 1970s studio sound with wide stereo keyboards (convention).
10. **Neighbours:** art rock, symphonic prog, Canterbury (jazzy), krautrock, prog metal, math rock.
11. **FL steps:** set time-signature changes in the playlist (FL supports per-pattern time signatures), write a main theme, then vary it across movements. Use Mellotron strings for transitions. **Fake mistakes:** odd meter with no sense of grouping (always define 2+2+3 etc.), quantized synth lead with no portamento.

#### 15. Math rock
1. **Identity:** late 1980s, USA and Japan. Pioneers: Polvo, Don Caballero, Slint, Bitch Magnet, Bastro, Ruins. Foundational: King Crimson's Red and Discipline, Slint's Spiderland. Japan: toe, tricot, LITE, Ling Tosite Sigure. Taiwan: Elephant Gym. Twinkly mathy emo: American Football, Tiny Moving Parts — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Math_rock). Modern crossover: Covet, Chon, Polyphia (convention). Refs: toe "Goodbye", tricot "Break", Don Caballero "Fire Back About Your New Baby's Sex" (convention). By ear: clean, tapped interlocking guitar lines in odd, shifting meters, with drums as a lead instrument.
2. **Tempo/feel:** "non-standard, frequently changing time signatures such as 5/4, 7/8, 11/8, or 13/8" (sourced). 110–180 BPM, often fast with a 16th-note pulse (convention).
3. **Harmony:** clean maj7/9/add9 voicings, open tunings, intervallic tapped patterns. "Tapping and loop pedals" (sourced). Example in A: Amaj9 - F#m11 - Dmaj7(#11) - E6/9 (convention).
4. **Melody:** mostly instrumental; vocals, when present, act as texture (sourced).
5. **Instruments:** clean guitars (often 2), bass that also taps, kit (drums "outsized role", sourced). DAW: clean DI guitar + compressor + plate reverb, slight chorus; MIDI guitar with legato/hammer-on articulations.
6. **Rhythm:** 7/8 and 5/4 grids (Q2).
7. **Bass:** syncopated counter-lines, sometimes tapped harmonics.
8. **Form:** sectional, riff-to-riff, few repeats. 3–6 min.
9. **Mix:** clean, articulate, dry-ish, with clear separation between guitars panned L/R (convention).
10. **Neighbours:** midwest emo (vocals, more 4/4), post-rock (slower, textural), prog (more keyboards, longer), Japanese math rock (brighter, pop vocals).
11. **FL steps:** set the pattern length to 7/8 (14 sixteenths), write 2 guitar lines that interlock (one plays on-grid notes, the other fills between), then change meter every 4–8 bars. **Fake mistakes:** distortion on the main parts, drums that play a plain 4/4 under an odd guitar riff.

#### 16. Garage rock revival (2000s) and garage rock roots
1. **Identity:** roots in 1960s US/Canadian garage rock (Pacific Northwest), peaking around 1966: "basic chord structures" on fuzzbox-distorted guitars, "bar chords or power chords", Farfisa organ, harmonica, tambourine, "nasal, growled, or shouted" vocals, and a deliberately "amateurish... raw" sound — [Wikipedia: Garage rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garage_rock_revival). (Note: the fetched URL returned the parent garage-rock content. The revival specifics below are convention.) Revival around 2001–2006: The Strokes (Is This It, 2001), The White Stripes (White Blood Cells/Elephant), The Hives, The Vines, Yeah Yeah Yeahs, The Black Keys, Arctic Monkeys (2006) (convention). By ear: lo-fi but punchy, two interlocking guitars (Strokes) or a fuzz guitar + drums duo (White Stripes), a compressed and distorted vocal, short songs.
2. **Tempo/feel:** 120–170 BPM (convention). Straight driving 8ths, sometimes a shuffle (Black Keys).
3. **Harmony:** I-IV-V, minor pentatonic fuzz riffs, Strokes-style single-note lines against chords. Example in E: E5-A5-B5, or the riff E-G-A-E (convention).
4. **Vocals:** distorted or telephone-filtered (Julian Casablancas), yelped (Karen O) (convention).
5. **Instruments:** cheap guitars into small amps, a vintage kit, Farfisa (sourced for 1960s). DAW: amp sim at crunch with an LPF, vocal bandpass 300 Hz–3 kHz + saturation (convention).
6. **Rhythm:** motorik-ish 8th hats + kick on 1, 3 and offbeats; Meg White-style minimal floor-tom/kick patterns (convention).
7. **Bass:** 8th roots; duos have no bass (octave-pedal guitar instead).
8. **Form:** V-C-V-C-solo-C. 2–3.5 min.
9. **Mix:** dry, mono-ish, analog-tape compression, room mics, little reverb (convention).
10. **Neighbours:** post-punk revival (Interpol, Franz Ferdinand: more angular, dance beat), blues-rock revival, 2020s "indie sleaze" nostalgia.
11. **FL steps:** a dry, small kit (FPC vintage), a single take per guitar (no quad tracking), saturation on the bus. **Fake mistakes:** huge reverb, polished quad-tracked guitars.

#### 17. Modern pop-rock and arena/stadium rock
1. **Identity:** arena rock dates to the late 1960s–70s (the Rolling Stones' 1969 US tour is credited as its birth). Grand Funk, Journey, Styx, Boston. 1980s hair metal. "Big voices, and really big guitars", "anthemic choruses", power ballads, keyboards — [Wikipedia: Arena rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_rock). Modern stadium/pop-rock: U2, Coldplay, The Killers, Imagine Dragons, Twenty One Pilots, Muse (the latter two "fuse... hard rock, electronica, hip-hop", [Alternative rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock)). 2024–26 current state: rock was among the fastest-growing US genres in 2025 per Luminate midyear data, ahead of Latin and country. Pop stars pivoting to rock: Olivia Rodrigo (late-90s/early-2000s guitar aesthetics), Charli XCX; Sleep Token, Geese, Wet Leg and Turnstile gaining traction — [RUSSH](https://www.russh.com/why-is-everyone-making-rock-albums-now/). Refs: "Don't Stop Believin'", "Livin' on a Prayer", "With or Without You", "Radioactive", "Good 4 U" (convention). By ear: a huge polished chorus, gang vocals, stomp-clap or four-on-floor anthem drums, and synths/pads blended with guitars.
2. **Tempo/feel:** 70–90 BPM for ballads and half-time anthems; 110–150 BPM for up-tempo (convention). Half-time choruses are common.
3. **Harmony:** I-V-vi-IV, vi-IV-I-V, IV-I-V-vi; power-ballad add9 piano chords; key-change up a whole step for the last chorus (1980s) (convention). In C: Am-F-C-G.
4. **Vocals:** belted, high-register chorus, "whoa-oh" hooks, stacked harmonies.
5. **Instruments:** guitars + synth pads + piano + programmed and live hybrid drums, sub-bass layered under the bass guitar (convention).
6. **Rhythm:** stomp-clap, four-on-floor floor-tom anthem, 1980s gated snare (Q2).
7. **Bass:** 8th-note pulse on roots (U2 style), synth-bass doubling.
8. **Form:** pop form: Intro 4–8, V 8–16, pre 4–8, C 8–16, post-chorus hook 4–8, V2, pre, C, bridge 8 (breakdown), final C x2 (with key change or extra gang vocals). 3–4 min (convention; see earlier modern-pop notes).
9. **Mix:** loud, wide, heavily compressed. Huge reverb on snare and vocals in the 1980s, tighter in modern mixes. Drum samples reinforced (convention).
10. **Neighbours:** AOR, power pop, post-grunge, emo-pop, pop-punk revival, hyper-rock (Auto-Tuned, glitchy, [Paste](https://www.pastemagazine.com/music/scene-report/the-emergence-of-hyper-rock)).
11. **FL steps:** layer a sample-replaced kick and snare with room reverb. Guitars sit behind the vocal (vocal-first mix), with a big pad bed and a post-chorus hook. Add a riser and a drop-out before the final chorus. **Fake mistakes:** guitars masking the vocal, no dynamic lift into the chorus.

### Inferences
- The quickest genre tells for a composer AI: (a) swing amount (rock and roll/blues shuffle about 66% vs everything else straight), (b) guitar gain (clean/jangle: indie, emo, math; crunch: classic, garage; high: hard rock, punk, grunge; fuzz plus reverb wall: shoegaze), (c) chord type (open triads: classic; 5ths: punk/grunge/hard; add9/maj7: emo, math, shoegaze, post-rock), (d) BPM band, and (e) form length (derived from the cards above).
- Bass role is a strong discriminator: post-punk = melodic lead bass; punk = root 8ths; post-rock = drone; math rock = counter-line; rock and roll = boogie walk (derived).
- Current-state (2025–26) relevance is highest for pop-punk and emo-pop (the Rodrigo/MGK lineage), shoegaze/nu-gaze/hyper-rock, post-punk talk-rock and modern arena pop-rock (derived from RUSSH/Paste/Stereogum).

- The garage rock revival page fetch returned 1960s garage-rock content, so revival-specific claims (Strokes etc.) are unverified conventions.
- The fetched Wikipedia text did not detail the modern neo-psych (Tame Impala, King Gizzard) or the 2020s UK/Irish post-punk sections.

## Q2. What is the canonical rock drum beat, and how does it vary by subgenre?

### Takeaway
Nearly all rock rests on one pattern: kick on 1 and 3, snare backbeat on 2 and 4, and 8th notes on the hi-hat or ride. Subgenres change tempo, which cymbal carries the 8ths, where the extra kicks fall, and whether the snare moves (half-time on 3, skank on every "&", d-beat kick on "&2"/"&4"). Shuffle genres use a 12-step triplet grid. Math and prog use odd-length grids, and post-rock varies density and velocity over minutes.

### Cited Findings
- Common punk/rock beat: hats on all 8ths, snare on 2 and 4, kick on 1, 3 and the "&" of 3. Most punk beats are "basic rock beats, but played with lots of energy and at a higher speed" — [drumhelper / Drumeo-network search results](https://drumhelper.com/learning-drums/punk-drum-beats-and-rhythms/); [freedrumlessons](https://freedrumlessons.com/drum-lessons/punk-drumming-lessons.php).
- D-beat: kick on 1 and 3 plus the "&" of 2 and the "&" of 4, snare on 2 and 4, crash on 1, 8th hats. Developed by Discharge's Terry "Tezz" Roberts around 1979, drawing on Motörhead (and Buzzcocks). The term was coined in 1988 — [Wikipedia: D-beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-beat).
- Blues rock tends toward straight 8ths rather than blues triplets — [Wikipedia: Blues rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues_rock). Hard rock keeps "some swing in the back beat" — [Wikipedia: Hard rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock).
- Post-rock drums draw on krautrock motorik and use "minimalist patterns that prioritize mood over groove" — [Wikipedia: Post-rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-rock). Post-punk uses steady, mechanical drumming, including drum machines — [Wikipedia: Post-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-punk).
- Math rock meters: 5/4, 7/8, 11/8, 13/8 — [Wikipedia: Math rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Math_rock).

### Grids (16 steps = one 4/4 bar unless labeled; velocity guide: X ≈ 110–127, x ≈ 85–105, g ≈ 25–45, hats alternate X/x for downbeat/upbeat feel; velocities are convention)

**A. Canonical rock beat ("money beat")** — classic, hard, alt, indie, garage (sourced pattern, Drumeo-network)
```
HiHat X . x . | X . x . | X . x . | X . x .
Snare . . . . | X . . . | . . . . | X . . .
Kick  X . . . | . . . . | X . x . | . . . .
```
Variations (convention): kick on "&" of 2 (step 7) for push; ghost snare on step 8 or 15 (`g`); open hat on step 15 (`o`) into the next bar; ride instead of hat in choruses; crash on step 1 of each new section.

**B. Rock and roll / blues shuffle** — 12 steps per bar (12/8 triplet grid, 3 steps per beat). In FL either use a 12-step pattern or a 16th grid with swing at about 66% (convention)
```
Ride  x . x | x . x | x . x | x . x
Snare . . . | X . . | . . . | X . .
Kick  X . . | . . . | X . . | . . .
```
Rockabilly train beat (convention): snare 16ths with accents on 2 and 4: `Snare x g g g | X g g g | x g g g | X g g g`.

**C. Punk 8th beat** — the same as A at 160–200 BPM with crash/open hat on all 8ths in choruses. Guitars downpick 8ths on top (sourced tempo range: Orphiq, aggregator).

**D. Skank / "polka" punk beat** — hardcore, pop-punk choruses, skate punk (convention). Kick every beat, snare every "&"
```
Ride  X . x . | X . x . | X . x . | X . x .
Snare . . X . | . . X . | . . X . | . . X .
Kick  X . . . | X . . . | X . . . | X . . .
```

**E. D-beat** — crust, hardcore, Motörhead-style (sourced pattern, [Wikipedia: D-beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-beat))
```
HiHat x . x . | x . x . | x . x . | x . x .
Snare . . . . | X . . . | . . . . | X . . .
Kick  X . . . | . . x . | X . . . | . . x .
Crash X . . . | . . . . | . . . . | . . . .   (bar 1 of phrase)
```

**F. Half-time feel** — pop-punk pre-chorus, grunge/emo breakdown, modern rock anthem choruses (convention). Snare on beat 3 only
```
HiHat X . x . | X . x . | X . x . | X . x .
Snare . . . . | . . . . | X . . . | . . . .
Kick  X . . . | . . x . | . . . . | . . x .
```

## Q3. How are guitars, bass and drums arranged together (riff-based vs chord-based, doubling, wall of sound)?

### Takeaway
There are three arrangement models: (1) riff-based (hard rock, blues rock, grunge riffs), where guitar and bass play the same line in octaves and the kick follows the riff; (2) chord-based strumming (classic, punk, pop-punk, indie), where bass plays root 8ths locked to the kick and two rhythm guitars are doubled hard L/R; (3) texture-based (shoegaze, post-rock, emo/math), where guitars become layers or interlocking arpeggios and bass anchors with roots or drones. Dynamics come from adding or removing layers between sections (quiet-loud).

### Cited Findings
- Hard rock bass gives "backing for the rhythm and lead guitars"; riffs are repetitive and of varying complexity; metal riffs are "stand-alone melodies" — [Wikipedia: Hard rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock).
- Punk: "one or two guitars, bass, drums and vocals", bass with a "relentless, repetitive forced rhythm" — [Wikipedia: Punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock).
- Post-punk: a melodic bass let guitarists move beyond rock conventions — [Wikipedia: Post-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-punk).
- Grunge: "stop-start" and soft/loud alternation, "ponderous basslines" — [Wikipedia: Grunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grunge). Alternative rock uses quiet verses and explosive choruses — [Wikipedia: Alternative rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock).
- Shoegaze is a Spector-like wall of sound where "no instrument is distinguishable" — [Wikipedia: Shoegaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoegaze).
- Post-rock guitars act as noise generators or texture palettes, bass as drones or loops, and builds come from layering timbres — [Wikipedia: Post-rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-rock).
- Midwest emo uses cycling/arpeggiated guitar parts over chugging bass — [Wikipedia: Midwest emo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest_emo). Math rock uses tapping and loop pedals to build rhythmic layers — [Wikipedia: Math rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Math_rock).
- Double-tracked rhythm guitars: play the same part twice and pan the takes to opposite sides. Copy-paste does not work because it needs "unique waveforms" — [Music Guy Mixing](https://www.musicguymixing.com/double-tracking-rhythm-guitars/). Keep the split fully hard (100%) to open the center for vocal, bass, snare and kick — [Violet Recording (search snippet)](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-double-track-rhythm-guitars/).

### Inferences / practical layering templates (convention unless marked)
- **Standard band layout:** Kick + bass + snare + lead vocal in the center. Rhythm guitar double at 100% L/R. Lead guitar or solo center or slightly off (±20%). Keys or second texture guitar ±40–60%. Overheads in stereo.
- **Riff rock (hard/grunge):** bass doubles the guitar riff an octave below. The kick hits on riff accents and the snare stays on 2/4. Solo sections drop to one rhythm guitar plus a center lead.
- **Punk/pop-punk:** two guitars playing the same power chords (doubled), bass root 8ths, everything locked to the 8th grid. In pop-punk verses, palm-muted guitar + bass + kick/snare only; choruses add open chords, crash and gang vocals.
- **Classic/indie two-guitar interlock:** guitar 1 strums chords, guitar 2 plays single-note riffs or arpeggios in a higher register (Strokes/Stones model).
- **Quiet-loud (grunge/alt/emo):** verse = bass + single clean or chorused guitar + closed hat. Chorus = doubled distorted guitars + crash/ride + backing vocals. That is about 3–6 dB of perceived lift and a jump from 1 to 3+ layers (derived from sourced soft/loud principle).
- **Wall of sound (shoegaze):** 3–6 guitar layers with different fuzz/reverb settings across the stereo field, all sent to a shared long reverb. Vocal below the guitars. Bass on simple roots for low-end clarity.
- **Crescendo (post-rock):** add one layer per 8 bars: clean arpeggio > bass drone > toms > second guitar tremolo melody > distortion + crash > strings/glock octave doubling.

## Q4. How do you emulate rock guitar and drums convincingly in a DAW (amp sims, double-tracking, MIDI humanization)?

### Takeaway
Rhythm guitars must be genuinely different performances (real takes, or MIDI guitar re-programmed with different timing and velocity), panned hard L/R, with slightly different amp or cab tones. MIDI drums need velocity variation (accents, ghosts), partial quantize (about 75%) or small timing offsets (about ±10 ticks), drummer-plausible limb logic, and a real room. Copy-pasted guitars, 100%-quantized fixed-velocity drums and over-randomization are what make a mix sound fake.

### Cited Findings
- Guitar doubling: vary the pickup or tone knob between takes (bridge vs neck/bridge blend), vary amp EQ/gain slightly, change individual notes on some chords, and pan the takes to opposite sides. Comping a duplicated take from different sections works as a fallback; plain duplicate + pan does not — [Music Guy Mixing](https://www.musicguymixing.com/double-tracking-rhythm-guitars/).
- Amp sims: use slightly different amp models or cab settings per side (e.g. AmpliTube, Guitar Rig, BIAS FX with different models L and R), or run the two takes through different cab sims and EQ them differently — [search summary of Violet Recording / Music Guy Mixing](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-record-guitar-with-an-amp-sim/).
- MIDI drums: quantize strength about 75% rather than 100%. Parallel-compress the drum bus with about 10 dB of gain reduction on the aux, blended in — [Mastering.com](https://mastering.com/program-realistic-midi-drums/).
- Humanization: vary velocity for accents; DAW randomize helps, but overuse sounds unrealistic. Push or pull notes about ±10 ticks. Include ghost notes, accents and flams — [Splice blog (search summary)](https://splice.com/blog/?p=5091); [Native Instruments blog](https://blog.native-instruments.com/midi-drums).
- Shoegaze texture tools: reverse reverb, dense "soft focus" reverb with parallel delays (250 ms/380 ms), fuzz, tremolo-bar-strummed reverb — [Keeley Loomer listing](https://reverb.com/item/3489147-keeley-loomer-fuzz-reverb-cme-exclusive).
- Grunge tone: DS-1/DS-2, Big Muff, Small Clone, Marshall, rolled-off treble — [Wikipedia: Grunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grunge). Psych: fuzz, wah, feedback, flanging, backwards tape — [Wikipedia: Psychedelic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_rock).

### Inferences / FL Studio workflow (convention unless marked)
**Guitar chain (FL):**
- Source: record a DI, or use a MIDI guitar VST (Ample Guitar / AGML Lite free, MusicLab RealStrat/RealLPC, Impact Soundworks Shreddage). These have palm-mute, slide and strum keyswitches.
- Amp: free Ignite Emissary/NadIR, ML Sound Lab, Neural DSP, Guitar Rig, AmpliTube. FL stock: the "Hardcore" guitar multi-FX, Fruity Fast Dist/Distructor for drive, and Fruity Convolver loading a cab IR.
- Gain staging: less gain than you think for doubled rhythm (stacking adds perceived saturation).
- EQ: HPF guitars about 80–120 Hz (leave the lows to bass/kick), LPF about 8–12 kHz to remove fizz (grunge lower, about 6–8 kHz, derived from "rolled-off treble"). A small dip around 2–4 kHz can make room for the vocal.

**MIDI guitar realism:**
- Strum offset: stagger chord notes 5–30 ms (downstroke low→high string, upstroke reversed), alternating with the 8th-note strum direction.
- Velocity: downstrokes slightly louder than upstrokes. Palm-muted notes are short (about 1/16 to 1/8 length).
- Program L and R parts separately with different random timing (±5–15 ms) and velocity seeds. Never clone them.
- Leads: pitch-bend automation for bends (whole step = +2 semitones with the bend range set accordingly) plus vibrato through the mod wheel or LFO. Slides between notes. Avoid perfectly quantized solos.

**Drums (FL):**
- Use FPC with an acoustic rock kit, or a sample-based VST (free: MT Power Drum Kit 2, Steven Slate Drums 5 Free; paid: Superior Drummer 3, EZdrummer 3, Addictive Drums 2), preferably with round-robin samples.
- Velocity bands: kick 100–120; snare backbeat 110–127 with ghosts 25–45; hats accented on downbeats 95–110, offbeats 70–85; crash 110–127. Rimshot backbeats in loud sections.
- Timing: quantize about 75% or offset ±10 ticks (sourced). Push hats slightly ahead for punk/pop-punk drive, lay the snare slightly back (5–15 ms) for grunge/classic "pocket".
- Limb logic: the right hand plays either hats, ride or crash, not two at once (except with a crash+kick on 1 and the hand moving). No hi-hat notes under tom fills except the pedal hat (left foot). Fills resolve to a crash + kick on the next downbeat. Choke crashes on stops.
- Room: send overheads/kit to a short room reverb (0.6–1.2 s) and parallel-compress the drum bus (about 10 dB gain reduction, sourced Mastering.com). 1980s arena: gated reverb on the snare.
- Tempo: for classic or garage feel, add tiny tempo drift (±1–2 BPM pushes into choruses) via tempo automation; modern pop-rock and pop-punk stay on the grid.

**Bass:**
- DI + amp sim, or a parallel distortion channel high-passed above about 300 Hz blended under a clean low end. Pick attack for punk/post-punk, fingerstyle for classic/blues. Tight with the kick (same grid positions, same humanize offsets).

**Common fake tells (summary):**
1. Copy-pasted L/R guitars that collapse in mono.
2. Fixed-velocity, 100%-quantized drums with impossible limb overlaps.
3. Too much gain or a scooped "metal" tone on non-metal genres.
4. Unbent, unvibratoed MIDI solos.
5. No dynamic difference between verse and chorus.
6. Reverb on everything except where the genre wants it (shoegaze/post-rock want lots; punk/garage want little).
