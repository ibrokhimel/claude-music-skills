# Metal family: genre recipe cards for FL Studio (heavy metal/NWOBHM, speed, thrash, death, black, doom/stoner/sludge, power, symphonic, nu metal, metalcore, deathcore, djent/prog, industrial, trap metal)

Conventions used in these notes
- Numbers are marked (sourced) with a link, (convention) for widely used practice I could not tie to a single source, or (derived) for arithmetic (for example MIDI note numbers).
- MIDI numbers use 60 = middle C = C4 (scientific pitch). **The FL Studio piano roll labels MIDI 60 as "C5"**, so add 1 to every octave number below when reading FL's piano roll.
- Lowest-string pitches by tuning (derived): E standard E2 = MIDI 40; Drop D D2 = 38; D standard D2 = 38; Drop C C2 = 36; C standard C2 = 36; B standard / 7-string standard B1 = 35; Drop A A1 = 33; A standard 7-string A1 = 33; 8-string F#1 = 30 (F1 = 29 for drop/F tunings).
- Power chord in MIDI = root + 7 semitones (+ 12 optional). On a drop tuning the lowest three strings give root-5th-octave under one finger, so drop-tuned riffs move parallel 1-5-8 shapes around chromatically (convention).
- Grid notation: 16 steps = one 4/4 bar of 16th notes; X accent, x normal, g ghost, . rest, o open hat; "Cym" = ride/crash/china lane as named.

## 1. What tunings, scales/modes, riff types and tempos define each subgenre? (recipe cards)

### Takeaway
Metal subgenres are told apart mostly by four audible levers: tuning depth (E/Eb standard for classic and power metal, D/C for thrash-to-death, B/A/7-8 string for nu metal, metalcore, deathcore and djent), right-hand technique (gallop vs palm-muted chug vs un-muted tremolo picking), drum engine (rock beat/gallop vs skank/thrash beat vs double-kick 16ths vs blast beat vs half-time breakdown vs hip-hop beat), and vocal type (clean high tenor vs shout vs growl vs shriek vs rap). Harmony across the family stays modal-minor: Aeolian and Phrygian, with tritone/chromatic riff movement and pedal points.

### Cited Findings
Family-wide harmony and tempo
- Heavy metal riffs rest on three harmonic traits: modal scale progressions, tritone and chromatic progressions, and pedal points; traditional metal favours Aeolian and Phrygian. Aeolian progressions cited: i-bVI-bVII, i-bVII-(bVI), i-bVI-iv-bVII; Phrygian progressions imply the i-bII relationship (i-bII-i, i-bII-III, i-bII-VII). Pentatonic and blues-derived features are also common — [Wikipedia: Heavy metal music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music)
- Metal tempos run from ballad tempos (quarter = 60 bpm) to extreme blast-beat tempos (quarter = 350 bpm) (sourced) — [Wikipedia: Heavy metal music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music)

NWOBHM / traditional heavy metal
- NWOBHM bands fused classic heavy metal with the immediacy of pub rock and punk; it was promoted as a movement in the late 1970s; Venom and Raven pushed it toward more extreme results — [Wikipedia: NWOBHM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_wave_of_British_heavy_metal)
- The metal "gallop" is a bass-drum figure of one eighth note followed by two sixteenths, named after a horse's canter — [search summary of drum-lesson sources incl. Drumeo/drumlessons.com](https://freedrumlessons.com/drum-lessons/heavy-metal-drumming.php)

Thrash / speed
- Thrash: fast tempos, low-register complex riffs, high-register solos, double-bass drumming; rhythm guitars heavily distorted power chords, often palm-muted for tightness; vocals range from melodic singing to shouts/screams — [Wikipedia: Thrash metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrash_metal)
- Thrash riffs often use chromatic scales and emphasize the tritone and diminished intervals rather than single-scale riffing; "Master of Puppets" intro is a chromatic descent then a tritone-based ascent. Solos use shredding: sweep picking, legato, alternate/tremolo picking, string skipping, tapping — [Wikipedia: Thrash metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrash_metal)
- Power metal's forerunner is speed metal; power metal combines traditional heavy metal with speed metal — [Wikipedia: Power metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_metal)

Death metal
- Heavily distorted, low-tuned guitars; palm muting and tremolo picking; deep growls; double kick and blast beats; minor keys or atonality; abrupt tempo, key and meter changes; chromatic chord progressions — [Wikipedia: Death metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_metal)
- Old-school death metal: slower tempos, simpler structures, fewer blasts. Slam (brutal) death metal: mid-tempo, breakdowns, palm-muted riffing, hip-hop-influenced vocal and drum rhythms; Suffocation's "Liege of Inveracity" breakdown credited as the first slam riff. Melodic death metal adds NWOBHM-style harmonized guitar riffs over death metal distortion, fast double-bass and occasional blasts — [Wikipedia: Death metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_metal); [Wikipedia: Melodic death metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melodic_death_metal)

Black metal
- Fast tempos, shrieked vocals, heavily distorted guitars played with fast UN-muted tremolo picking and power chords, raw lo-fi recording, unconventional song structures, emphasis on atmosphere; Norwegian style favours high-pitched/trebly tones; dissonance and the tritone (flat fifth) are common; guitar solos and low tunings are rare; drumming relies on double-bass and blast beats at tempos that sometimes approach 300 bpm (sourced) — [Wikipedia: Black metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_metal)
- Raw black metal amplifies lo-fi production with higher-pitched guitars/vocals and more tremolo/blasts (Ildjarn, Gorgoroth, Darkthrone). Symphonic black metal adds orchestral instruments/keyboards (Emperor, Dimmu Borgir). Atmospheric styles are slow-to-mid paced with rare blasts and slowly developing repetitive riffs. Blackgaze blends blasts and shrieks with shoegaze guitar textures — [Wikipedia: Black metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_metal)

Doom / stoner / sludge
- Doom: slower tempos, low-tuned guitars, thick heavy sound, blues-rooted scales, minor tonality with much dissonance (especially the tritone), repetitive rhythms with little regard for harmonic progression; guitars and bass in unison form a bass-heavy wall. Traditional doom uses higher tunings and is less slow — [Wikipedia: Doom metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_metal)
- Sludge = doom + hardcore punk: slow and heavy with brief hardcore passages, strings heavily distorted with large amounts of feedback; drummers may use d-beat or double kick in fast passages — [Wikipedia: Doom metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_metal); [Wikipedia: Sludge metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sludge_metal)
- Stoner rock/metal: slow-to-mid tempo, heavily distorted groove-laden bass-heavy sound, melodic vocals, "retro" production, psychedelia — [Wikipedia: Stoner rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoner_rock)

Power / symphonic
- Power metal: fast riffs and drumming, twin melodies, operatic singing; vocalist-centric with clean high-register tenor vocals and wide range; traditional branch (Manowar, Helloween) mid-to-fast with anthemic choruses; symphonic branch (Rhapsody, Nightwish) late 1990s, lighter and uplifting — [Wikipedia: Power metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_metal)
- Symphonic metal: workstation keyboards and orchestras are key; guitars, bass, drums may play simpler parts under complex keyboard/orchestral parts; budget bands use workstation presets (strings, choirs, piano, organ); others use pre-recorded or virtual orchestras sequenced in a DAW; often a soprano female lead, choirs; growls used less (Epica's soprano-vs-growl contrast is an exception) — [Wikipedia: Symphonic metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphonic_metal)

Nu metal
- Combines metal with hip-hop, funk, industrial, grunge; rarely features solos; heavily syncopated rhythm; often down-tuned seven-strings; vocals sung, rapped, screamed; bassists funk-influenced and drummers hip-hop-influenced; blast beats and double bass uncommon (Joey Jordison, Matt McDonough exceptions); common time; Dorian, Aeolian or Phrygian modes — [Wikipedia: Nu metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nu_metal)
- Korn play in A standard on 7-strings (A D G C F A D) — [Killer Guitar Rigs](https://killerguitarrigs.com/?p=1793). Same source claims many Slipknot songs are in Drop A on 6-strings; I could not cross-verify (see Gaps).

Metalcore
- Aggressive verses, melodic choruses, slow intense breakdowns; low-tuned percussive riffs; stop-start rhythm guitar; heavy palm muting; double-bass; highly produced sound; pick scrapes and dissonant chords in breakdowns; tunings usually between Drop D and A, with 7/8-strings not uncommon; clean/harsh vocal alternation, often clean in chorus or bridge; modern metalcore associated with autotuned clean vocals — [Wikipedia: Metalcore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metalcore)
- Melodic metalcore = metalcore + melodic death metal (melodic riffs, screams, breakdowns, clean singing) — [Wikipedia: Melodic death metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melodic_death_metal)

Deathcore
- Fusion of metalcore, hardcore and death metal: fast tempos, down-tuned guitars, death metal riffs, tremolo picking, double bass and blasts, defined by breakdowns; the characteristic breakdown is single palm-muted notes repeated on low open strings; vocals alternate low growls and high screams/"pig squeals"; Suffocation, Dying Fetus, Internal Bleeding cited as influences — [Wikipedia: Deathcore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deathcore); [Wikipedia: Death metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_metal)

Djent / progressive
- Djent (from Meshuggah; popularized late 2000s by Periphery, Tesseract, Animals as Leaders, Vildhjarta): high-attack, palm-muted, syncopated riffs, often polymetric, on extended-range guitars (7, 8, 9+ strings); virtuosic solos; "CAFO" by Animals as Leaders cited as an example — [Wikipedia: Progressive metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_metal); [Wikipedia: Djent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djent)
- Meshuggah used Bb standard on 7-strings for "Future Breed Machine"; the most common 8-string tuning extends standard tuning with low B and F# (F#1-E6) — [Killer Guitar Rigs](https://killerguitarrigs.com/?p=1793); [Strandberg](https://strandbergguitars.com/7-and-8-string-guitar-tunings-with-strandberg)

Industrial metal
- Drum machines replacing or supplementing drums, samplers looping metal riffs, synths/sequencers, repetitive mechanical riffs, processed/distorted vocals; Ministry (The Land of Rape and Honey, 1989), Godflesh (1988 EP), Nine Inch Nails (Broken 1992, The Downward Spiral 1994), Rammstein, Fear Factory; Neue Deutsche Harte = German-language industrial metal — [Wikipedia: Industrial metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_metal)
- Cyber metal (Fear Factory-coined, The Kovenant): adds EBM/aggrotech elements with more melodic, less repetitive riffs — [Wikipedia: Industrial metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_metal)

Trap metal (modern crossover)
- Trap metal (aka ragecore, death rap, industrial trap, scream rap) originated early 2010s US from trap, heavy metal, industrial and nu metal; typical instruments are vocals, a TR-808 drum machine, and occasional guitar; not the same as rage — [Wikipedia: Rap metal § Trap metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trap_metal)
- Characterized by heavily distorted 808s (not clean 808s), tuned-down guitars, screamed vocals, industrial/nu-metal soundscapes, hip-hop flows; Ghostemane blends horrorcore, industrial, trap and black metal — [eMastered: dark trap](https://emastered.com/es/blog/what-is-dark-trap-music)

### Inferences — the recipe cards
(Every card follows the 11-point format. BPM ranges are (convention) unless a link is given; grids are (convention) idioms I wrote from the sourced descriptions.)

#### Card A. Traditional heavy metal / NWOBHM
1. Identity: Birmingham/UK roots (Black Sabbath 1970) then NWOBHM late 1970s-early 1980s (Iron Maiden, Judas Priest, Saxon, Diamond Head, Motorhead-adjacent) [NWOBHM wiki]. References: Iron Maiden "The Trooper", "Run to the Hills"; Judas Priest "Breaking the Law"; Diamond Head "Am I Evil?". By ear: galloping bass/guitar, twin harmonized lead guitars in 3rds, high clean wailing vocals, E standard brightness.
2. Tempo/feel: 110-180 bpm, default 160 (convention); 4/4 straight 16ths; gallop drives it; shuffled 12/8 for blues-rooted Sabbath-style tracks.
3. Harmony: E or A minor (open-string friendly, E standard). Aeolian i-bVI-bVII (Em-C-D) and i-bVII-bVI (Em-D-C) [heavy metal wiki]; pedal-point riffs (alternate open E with moving notes). Power chords; harmonic rhythm 1-2 chords per bar.
4. Melody/vocals: high tenor clean vocals with vibrato, sirens; twin guitar leads harmonized in diatonic 3rds/6ths; natural minor plus harmonic minor (raised 7th) in solos (convention).
5. Sounds: two guitars (E standard, medium gain, less saturated than modern), bass audible with pick, acoustic kit with roomy snare. DAW: guitar VST (Ample Metal/Shreddage class) or MIDI-to-amp-sim; FL stock fallback: "Hardcore" guitar multi-FX or Distructor amp/cab after a saw/pluck (sounds synthetic; use only as sketch).
6. Rhythm (gallop, 160 bpm):
   `Kick  X . x x | . . . . | X . x x | . . . .`
   `Snare . . . . | X . . . | . . . . | X . . .`
   `Hat   x . x . | x . x . | x . x . | x . x . `   (crash on step 1 of each phrase)
   Guitar/bass gallop cell per beat = 8th + two 16ths (steps 1, 3, 4) [gallop source]. Full-gallop kick on every beat (`X . x x` x4) for a driving chorus.
7. Bass: picked gallops doubling the guitar root, occasionally independent lines (Steve Harris style).
8. Form: intro riff (4-8) / verse (16) / pre (8) / chorus (8) / verse / chorus / twin-lead + solo section (16-32) / chorus x2 / outro; 4-6 min (convention).
9. Mix: moderate gain, wide double-tracked rhythm guitars, natural reverb on snare and vocal, less compressed than modern metal.
10. Neighbours: Speed metal = NWOBHM at 170-220 bpm with more double-kick (Judas Priest "Painkiller", Motorhead, early Metallica); Power metal (Card G) adds epic melody; Thrash (Card B) darker, chromatic, palm-muted.
11. FL steps: set 160 bpm; program gallop kick in FPC; write E5-power-chord gallop riff (E2-B2-E3) and harmonized lead in 3rds; double-track guitars hard L/R. Fake-sounding mistakes: identical copy-pasted guitar L/R (no real double), modern 200 Hz-scooped high-gain tone, machine-gun 127-velocity drums.

#### Card B. Thrash metal (and speed metal)
1. Identity: early-1980s Bay Area/US East Coast and Germany (Metallica, Slayer, Megadeth, Anthrax, Exodus, Kreator, Sodom, Testament) [thrash wiki]. References: Metallica "Master of Puppets", Slayer "Angel of Death", Megadeth "Holy Wars". By ear: fast palm-muted down-picked 16th/8th chugs on the low E, chromatic/tritone riffs, skank ("thrash") beat, shouted vocals, shred solos.
2. Tempo/feel: 160-240 bpm, default 190 (convention); thrash/skank beat on 8ths; half-time "mosh" sections at the same bpm; straight.
3. Harmony: E Phrygian / E minor with chromatic passing tones; tritone E-Bb; riffs built from open E pedal with power-chord stabs on F, G, Bb (convention, consistent with [thrash wiki] chromatic/tritone description). Example: E5 pedal 16ths then F5-E5 (i-bII) then Bb5 (tritone). Harmonic rhythm: riff-driven, chord changes on 8th notes.
4. Melody/vocals: barked/shouted mid-high vocals; solos with whammy dives, chromatic shred, sweeps [thrash wiki].
5. Sounds: E standard or Eb/D standard (Slayer later lowered tunings [thrash wiki]); tight high-gain, mids present; bass picked, gritty; dry kit with ping ride.
6. Rhythm (skank/thrash beat, 190 bpm):
   `Kick  X . . . | x . . . | X . . . | x . . . `
   `Snare . . x . | . . x . | . . x . | . . x . `
   `Cym   x . x . | x . x . | x . x . | x . x . ` (ride or crash on every 8th)
   The skank beat = sped-up 2/4 polka; in the US called the "Slayer" or "thrash" beat [Wikipedia: Blast beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_beat). Double-time variant: double-kick 16ths under a snare on 5 and 13 (see Q2).
7. Bass: doubles guitar riff in unison, pick attack.
8. Form: intro riff / verse x2 / chorus / verse / chorus / tempo-shifted middle section with multiple riffs and solos / recap; 4-8 min (convention).
9. Mix: drier than modern metal, mid-forward guitars, scooped-mid sound only in late-80s Metallica (convention).
10. Neighbours: speed metal = less chromatic, more NWOBHM-melodic; crossover thrash = shorter, punk forms; groove metal (Pantera) = mid-tempo syncopated chugs, lower tunings [Wikipedia: Groove metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groove_metal).
11. FL steps: 190 bpm; FPC skank beat; riff on E2 pedal with 16th palm mutes (short notes, 60-80% length) and accented power-chord hits; quad-track (Q4). Mistakes: legato-length MIDI notes (palm mutes must be short and slightly velocity-varied), no tempo change for the mid-section, bass buried.

#### Card C. Death metal (OSDM, brutal/slam, melodic death, tech-death)
1. Identity: mid-1980s Florida (Death, Morbid Angel, Obituary, Cannibal Corpse) and Sweden (Entombed, Dismember; Gothenburg melodeath: At the Gates, In Flames) [death metal wiki]. References: Death "Pull the Plug", Cannibal Corpse "Hammer Smashed Face", At the Gates "Blinded by Fear". By ear: guttural growls, low tuning, tremolo + palm-muted chromatic riffs, blast beats alternating with double-kick and slower groove sections.
2. Tempo/feel: 100-260 bpm with abrupt changes inside one song (sourced trait), default 200 for blast sections and 100-120 for groove/slam (convention).
3. Harmony: minor/atonal, chromatic clusters; Phrygian + Locrian colour; tritone and b2 everywhere; example in Drop C/C standard: C5-Db5-C5-Gb5 (i-bII-i-bV) (convention, consistent with sourced "chromatic progressions").
4. Vocals: low growls; leads are chaotic whammy solos (OSDM) or harmonized melodic lines (melodeath).
5. Sounds: D/C standard or lower (convention), very high gain, scooped-to-neutral mids; triggered kicks; tight bass often distorted.
6. Rhythm: traditional blast (Q2), bomb blast, double-kick 16ths, OSDM d-beat/skank. Slam: half-time snare on 9 with kick syncopations doubling guitar.
7. Bass: unison with guitar, sometimes independent in tech-death.
8. Form: riff chains (A-B-C-B-D...), less verse-chorus; 3-5 min (convention).
9. Mix: dense, compressed, clicky kicks (Q4); OSDM roomier and saturated (Swedish "HM-2 buzzsaw" tone, convention).
10. Neighbours: melodeath = NWOBHM harmonies over death drums [melodeath wiki]; blackened death = more tremolo and black-metal progressions [death wiki]; slam = mid-tempo breakdowns and hip-hop-ish drum rhythms [death wiki]; grindcore = very short songs, constant blasts (convention).
11. FL steps: write a 2-bar tremolo riff (32nd/16th repeated notes) in C Phrygian, a chromatic palm-muted riff, and a slam riff; switch tempo via FL tempo automation; layer growl-free (instrumental) or use placeholder. Mistakes: every bar at the same tempo/feel, blast with no variation in cymbal velocity, guitar tremolo notes all at identical velocity.

#### Card D. Black metal (second wave, raw, atmospheric, symphonic)
1. Identity: first wave (Venom, Bathory, Hellhammer, early 1980s), second wave Norway early 1990s (Mayhem, Darkthrone, Burzum, Emperor, Immortal) [black wiki]. References: Darkthrone "Transilvanian Hunger", Mayhem "Freezing Moon", Emperor "I Am the Black Wizards". By ear: shrieks, fast un-muted tremolo-picked chords, blast beats, trebly thin lo-fi wash, long repetitive riffs.
2. Tempo/feel: blasts up to ~300 bpm (sourced, [black wiki]); typical 160-220 for blast sections and 90-130 for mid-paced "pagan" sections (convention). Straight.
3. Harmony: minor with dissonance and tritone [black wiki]; open-string drones with moving chord shapes (convention); progressions such as i-bVI-bVII-i in tremolo (convention); harmonic minor for "evil" melodic cadences (convention).
4. Vocals: high rasp/shriek; melodies played by tremolo guitar lines rather than voice.
5. Sounds: E/Eb standard (low tunings rare [black wiki]), trebly high-gain with little low end, bass buried, kick low in mix, often keyboard pads (symphonic).
6. Rhythm: traditional blast (Q2); skank beat for mid-sections; double-kick 16ths with crash on quarters for "wall" sections.
7. Bass: follows roots, often barely audible.
8. Form: long riff cycles, each riff repeated 4-16 times, minimal choruses, 6-10 min (convention); intros/outros with ambient synth.
9. Mix: lo-fi, hiss, reverb wash, mids/highs dominant, low end thin (sourced character [black wiki]).
10. Neighbours: atmospheric/Cascadian (slower, folk/doom elements); DSBM (slow, depressive, clean guitars); blackgaze (shoegaze guitar, major-key brightness) [black wiki]; symphonic black (orchestral keys) [black wiki].
11. FL steps: 200 bpm; tremolo riff = 16th notes (or 32nds at half the bpm) on chord shapes (root-5th-octave + minor 3rd/b2 colour), not palm-muted; traditional blast; HPF guitars higher (~150-200 Hz) and push 2-5 kHz (convention); add hiss/tape saturation. Mistakes: polished modern clicky kicks and tight chugs (that is death/core, not black metal), palm muting.

#### Card E. Doom / stoner / sludge
1. Identity: Black Sabbath roots; traditional doom (Pentagram, Saint Vitus, Candlemass), funeral doom, stoner (Kyuss, Sleep, Electric Wizard), sludge (Melvins, Eyehategod, Crowbar) [doom, stoner, sludge wikis]. References: Black Sabbath "Black Sabbath", Candlemass "Solitude", Sleep "Dragonaut", Electric Wizard "Funeralopolis", Eyehategod (NOLA sludge, album "Take as Needed for Pain"), Crowbar "All I Had (I Gave)". By ear: crawling tempo, massive fuzz/feedback, tritone riffs, ring-out chords.
2. Tempo: funeral doom 40-60, doom 50-80, stoner 70-120 often swung/shuffled, sludge 60-90 with sudden hardcore bursts at 160-200 (convention; slow-tempo trait sourced [doom wiki]).
3. Harmony: blues-rooted minor pentatonic + b5 [doom wiki]; tritone; examples in C standard/Drop C: C5-Gb5-F5 (i-bV-iv), C5-Eb5-F5-Gb5 (blues ascent).
4. Vocals: doom: clean, mournful/operatic (Candlemass); stoner: melodic, laid-back; sludge: screamed/hardcore.
5. Sounds: big fuzz (Big Muff-style) into tube amp, bass fuzzed and unison with guitar [doom wiki]; huge room kit; organ/Mellotron in stoner.
6. Rhythm (doom at 65 bpm):
   `Kick  X . . . | . . . . | x . x . | . . . . `
   `Snare . . . . | . . . . | X . . . | . . . . `   (or snare on 5 and 13 at 2x slower feel)
   `Cym   X . . . | x . . . | x . . . | x . . . ` (crash/ride on quarters; tom fills at phrase ends)
   Stoner shuffle: 12/8 or swing ~66% (convention).
7. Bass: fuzzed, unison with guitar; sustain notes to the next change.
8. Form: few riffs repeated long; 6-15 min songs (convention); sludge inserts 4-8 bar hardcore bursts.
9. Mix: warm, saturated, low-mid heavy, roomy; less tight than modern metal (convention).
10. Neighbours: doom vs sludge = sludge adds hardcore screams, feedback, d-beat bursts [sludge/doom wikis]; stoner = groovier, psychedelic, retro, melodic vocals [stoner wiki]; traditional doom = higher tuning, not as slow [doom wiki].
11. FL steps: 60-70 bpm; long sustained notes (full length, let ring); bass fuzz via Fruity Fast Dist/Distructor into low-pass; add feedback swells with pitch automation; drums with long room reverb. Mistakes: 16th-note palm-mute chugs (wrong idiom), over-tight gating, clean thin bass.

#### Card F. Power metal (and speed-adjacent)
1. Identity: 1980s Europe/US; Helloween (Germany), Manowar, Blind Guardian, Gamma Ray, Stratovarius, Sonata Arctica, DragonForce, Sabaton [power wiki]. References: Helloween "I Want Out", Blind Guardian "Valhalla", DragonForce "Through the Fire and Flames". By ear: major-key-ish triumphant choruses, high tenor clean vocals, nonstop double-kick, twin/keyboard leads.
2. Tempo: 140-200 bpm, default 170 (convention); straight; double-kick 16ths throughout fast parts.
3. Harmony: natural minor with heroic major relative turns and harmonic minor (neoclassical); i-bVI-bVII-i (Am-F-G-Am) and bVI-bVII-i; final chorus modulation up a whole step (convention).
4. Vocals: high tenor, wide range, clean [power wiki]; gang/choir backing; neoclassical shred and keyboard solos.
5. Sounds: E/Eb standard or D standard (convention), tight but less extreme gain, bright synth strings/choir pads, harpsichord/lead synth.
6. Rhythm (170 bpm):
   `Kick  x x x x | x x x x | x x x x | x x x x `
   `Snare . . . . | X . . . | . . . . | X . . . `
   `Cym   x . . . | x . . . | x . . . | x . . . ` (crash/ride on quarters)
7. Bass: root 8ths, follows kick or guitar.
8. Form: intro (often orchestral) / verse / pre / big chorus / verse / chorus / solo trade-off (guitar vs keys) / breakdown chorus (half-time, vocals only + kick) / key-change final chorus; 4-7 min (convention).
9. Mix: polished, bright, big reverb on vocals/orchestra, kicks clicky enough to be heard at 16ths.
10. Neighbours: symphonic power (Rhapsody, Nightwish) with orchestra; traditional/US power heavier, mid-tempo [power wiki].
11. FL steps: kick roll in FPC with velocity alternation (e.g., 110/95) so 16ths breathe; writing a singable chorus melody in high register; add strings/choir layer. Mistakes: kick 16ths at constant 127 (machine-gun), no melody focus.

#### Card G. Symphonic metal (gothic/symphonic, symphonic power)
1. Identity: late 1990s Netherlands/Finland (Nightwish, Within Temptation, Epica, After Forever), Therion [symphonic wiki]. References: Nightwish "Ghost Love Score", Within Temptation "Ice Queen", Epica "Cry for the Moon". By ear: orchestral strings/choir carrying the main melody, operatic soprano, simpler guitars underneath.
2. Tempo: 90-170 bpm, default 120-140 (convention).
3. Harmony: minor keys, cinematic progressions i-bVI-bIII-bVII (Dm-Bb-F-C), Picardy/major lifts; harmonic minor (convention).
4. Vocals: soprano/operatic female lead, baritone male, choirs; occasional growls (Epica beauty-and-beast) [symphonic wiki].
5. Sounds: orchestra (strings, brass, choir, timpani), workstation presets acceptable [symphonic wiki]; DAW: orchestral libraries (BBC Symphony Orchestra Discover, Spitfire LABS) (convention); FL stock: FL Keys/Sytrus pads only as sketch.
6. Rhythm: mid-tempo rock beat with double-kick fills or Card F grid; timpani accents on 1 and 9.
7. Bass: roots locked to kick.
8. Form: orchestral intro / verse / chorus / verse / chorus / orchestral bridge / climax chorus; 4-6 min; epics 10+ min (convention).
9. Mix: wide, reverberant, orchestra gets midrange space; guitars HPF'd and slightly lower than in other metal (convention).
10. Neighbours: symphonic black (harsh, blasts); symphonic power (faster, tenor lead); gothic metal (slower, darker baritone) [symphonic wiki].
11. FL steps: write orchestral parts first (strings ostinato in 8ths, choir pads), then guitar power chords doubling the low strings; leave 250-500 Hz room. Mistakes: guitars masking orchestra, choir pads with no articulation changes.

#### Card H. Nu metal (and 2020s nu-metal revival)
1. Identity: mid-late 1990s US (Korn, Deftones, Limp Bizkit, Slipknot, Linkin Park, System of a Down) [nu wiki]. References: Korn "Freak on a Leash", Slipknot "Duality", Linkin Park "One Step Closer", Deftones "My Own Summer". By ear: down-tuned 7-string syncopated chug riffs with rests, hip-hop drum grooves, rapped/whispered verses into screamed/sung choruses, DJ scratches/samples, no solos.
2. Tempo: 85-140 bpm, default 100-105 (convention); straight or slightly swung hip-hop feel (54-58% swing on hats, convention).
3. Harmony: Dorian, Aeolian, Phrygian [nu wiki]; one-note riffs on the low open string with b2 and b5 hits; dissonant high-string harmonics/bends; example in A standard 7-string: A1 chugs with Bb (b2) and Eb (b5) stabs.
4. Vocals: rap, sing, scream, whisper [nu wiki]; hooks in clean chorus.
5. Sounds: 7-string A/B tuning [Korn source], scooped heavy tone, clicky funk-slap or growly bass [nu wiki], turntables, samplers, synth textures.
6. Rhythm (100 bpm, hip-hop beat):
   `Kick  X . . x | . . X . | . . x . | . x . . `
   `Snare . . . . | X . . g | . . . . | X . . . `
   `Hat   x . x . | x . x . | x . x . | x . o . `
   Guitar chugs lock to kick syncopations with 16th rests (the "stop" is as important as the hit).
7. Bass: unison with guitar chugs, often slapped/pick-clank (Korn) or sub-heavy.
8. Form: pop-structured verse/chorus/verse/chorus/bridge-breakdown/chorus; quiet-loud dynamics; 3-4 min (convention).
9. Mix: big scooped guitars, sampled loops, hip-hop drum processing, sometimes lo-fi intros.
10. Neighbours: rap metal (more rap-rock riffs); 2020s revival (Tallah, Bad Omens, Sleep Token influence) merges nu metal with metalcore and R&B (see Card I/2025 trends).
11. FL steps: 100 bpm, FPC hip-hop beat with ghost snares; one-string riff with rests; add scratch FX and a filtered vocal-chop sample; quiet verse (clean guitar + bass + beat) to loud chorus. Mistakes: adding double-kick/blasts (nu metal rarely uses them [nu wiki]), guitar solos, constant wall of distortion with no dynamic contrast.

#### Card I. Metalcore (melodic metalcore, modern "nu-metalcore")
1. Identity: early-1990s hardcore crossover (Integrity, Earth Crisis, Converge), 2000s melodic metalcore (Killswitch Engage, As I Lay Dying, Trivium, Bullet for My Valentine), 2010s-2020s (Architects, Bring Me the Horizon, Parkway Drive, Wage War, Bad Omens, Spiritbox) [metalcore wiki]. References: Killswitch Engage "My Last Serenade", Architects "Doomsday", Bring Me the Horizon "Can You Feel My Heart", Bad Omens "Just Pretend". By ear: screamed verses, big clean sung chorus, chuggy breakdown with china cymbal, glossy produced sound.
2. Tempo: 120-200 bpm, default 150-160 for verses with half-time breakdowns at the same bpm (convention).
3. Harmony: minor key; Aeolian chorus progressions i-bVI-bIII-bVII (in Drop C: Cm-Ab-Eb-Bb); melodic-death-style harmonized riffs; dissonant chords and pick scrapes in breakdowns [metalcore wiki].
4. Vocals: harsh verses, clean (often autotuned) choruses [metalcore wiki].
5. Sounds: Drop D to A, 7/8-strings [metalcore wiki]; tight, very high-gain, boosted tone; triggered drums; synth pads, 808 drops and electronic FX in modern production.
6. Rhythm: verse = double-kick 16ths or "2-step" hardcore beat; chorus = driving 8th rock beat with crash; breakdown = half-time (Q2/Q3).
7. Bass: follows guitar, sub/808 drops in breakdowns (bass drop mentioned [breakdown wiki]).
8. Form: intro / verse (harsh) / pre / chorus (clean) / verse / chorus / bridge (clean or ambient) / breakdown / final chorus; 3-4 min (convention).
9. Mix: very polished, loud (modern masters often around -6 to -8 LUFS integrated, convention, unverified), quad-tracked guitars, sample-replaced drums.
10. Neighbours: deathcore = lower, growlier, blasts, slower breakdowns; post-hardcore = less metal riffing; melodic death = no breakdown focus.
11. FL steps: 150 bpm; write chorus melody + chords first; write verse riff on low string with accents; build breakdown at half-time (kick unison with chugs, snare on 9, china quarters); add 808 sub drop at breakdown start; FX risers/reverse cymbals. Mistakes: breakdown not half-time, clean chorus guitars too distorted to let vocal sit, no space (bars of silence/stops) before breakdowns.

#### Card J. Deathcore
1. Identity: mid-2000s US (Suicide Silence, Whitechapel, Job for a Cowboy, Despised Icon, Carnifex, Chelsea Grin), 2020s symphonic/blackened deathcore (Lorna Shore, Shadow of Intent) [deathcore wiki]. References: Suicide Silence "Unanswered", Whitechapel "This Is Exile", Lorna Shore "To the Hellfire". By ear: blasts + death riffs then extremely low, slow breakdowns of repeated open-string chugs; growls and pig squeals.
2. Tempo: 100-240 bpm, blasts 200+, breakdowns at half-time of song tempo or slowed tempo ramps (convention).
3. Harmony: chromatic/Phrygian; open low string (Drop G#/A/B on 6-7 strings) plus b2 and tritone hits; symphonic deathcore adds harmonic minor orchestral layers (convention).
4. Vocals: low growls, high screams, pig squeals [deathcore wiki].
5. Sounds: very low tunings (Drop A and lower, convention), ultra-tight boosted high gain, 100% sample-replaced drums common [Nail The Mix kick/sample sources], sub drops, orchestral/choir layers in modern bands.
6. Rhythm: bomb blast for verses; breakdown grid (Q3) with kick unison; "slam" variant with snare every beat 3.
7. Bass: unison, often doubled by sine sub on breakdown downbeats.
8. Form: riff-blast-breakdown cycles, multiple breakdowns, the last one slowest/heaviest; 3-5 min (convention).
9. Mix: dense, compressed, low end controlled by multiband/dynamic EQ; kicks with hard click.
10. Neighbours: metalcore (cleaner choruses, higher tuning); brutal death/slam (less hardcore-styled breakdowns, more technical riffs) [death wiki].
11. FL steps: write the breakdown first (single open-note chugs with syncopated rests); tempo-automate the last breakdown slower; layer sub sine on downbeat chugs. Mistakes: muddy low end (no HPF on guitars), chugs that are too long (must be staccato), no rests.

#### Card K. Djent / progressive metal
1. Identity: Meshuggah (Sweden, 1990s) then late-2000s online scene: Periphery, Tesseract, Animals as Leaders, Vildhjarta [prog/djent wikis]. References: Meshuggah "Bleed", Periphery "Icarus Lives!", Tesseract "Concealing Fate", Animals as Leaders "CAFO" [djent wiki]. By ear: tight high-attack palm-muted chugs (the "djent" sound) in odd groupings over a steady 4/4 snare/cymbal, extended-range low notes, ambient clean layers.
2. Tempo: 100-150 bpm, often felt half-time (convention); polymetric riffs resolving after 2-4+ bars [Premier Guitar polymeter](https://www.premierguitar.com/articles/26211-obsessive-progressive-how-to-decode-advanced-polymeters).
3. Harmony: low-string pedal with extended chords (add9, maj7#11, sus2) in clean sections; Phrygian/Locrian chug riffs; Lydian/Dorian colours in Periphery/Tesseract (convention).
4. Vocals: clean airy tenor vs harsh screams; often falsetto (convention).
5. Sounds: 7/8-strings (Bb standard 7, F#/F 8-string) [tuning sources]; noise-gated, boosted tight high gain; ambient delays/reverbs; programmed drums typical (convention).
6. Rhythm: hands keep 4/4 (crash/china quarters, snare on 9 for half-time) while kick follows guitar groupings (Q2 grid).
7. Bass: unison, often distorted bass with clean sub split.
8. Form: through-composed riff blocks with ambient interludes; 4-7 min (convention).
9. Mix: hyper-tight, edited, gated guitars, clicky kicks, wide ambient clean guitars.
10. Neighbours: prog metal (Dream Theater: odd meters, virtuosity, less chug); mathcore (chaotic, dissonant, hardcore-rooted [metalcore wiki]).
11. FL steps: set 4/4; draw a riff cycle of N 16ths (e.g., 5+5+3+3 = 16, or 7-7-7-7-4 over 2 bars [Premier Guitar]); copy kick to match guitar; keep snare on 9; gate the guitar track tightly. Mistakes: putting the snare on the odd grouping (loses the "groove over 4/4" illusion), sloppy MIDI note-ends (djent needs abrupt stops).

#### Card L. Industrial metal (incl. Neue Deutsche Harte, cyber metal)
1. Identity: late 1980s (Ministry, Godflesh), 1990s peak (NIN, White Zombie, Fear Factory, Rammstein, Marilyn Manson) [industrial wiki]. References: Ministry "Thieves", NIN "March of the Pigs", Rammstein "Du hast", Fear Factory "Replica". By ear: drum-machine-precise beats, looped riffs, synth/sampler noise, processed vocals, marching repetition.
2. Tempo: 100-140 bpm, default 120 (convention); quantized, no swing.
3. Harmony: one- or two-chord riffs, Phrygian b2, tritone; NDH uses simple minor triads and synth arps (convention).
4. Vocals: distorted/processed shouts [industrial wiki]; NDH deep baritone in German.
5. Sounds: drum machine, samplers, synths/sequencers, distorted guitars [industrial wiki]; FL stock: FPC with processed kits, 3x Osc/Sytrus/Harmor for arps/noise, Fruity Blood Overdrive/Distructor on drums.
6. Rhythm (Rammstein-style 120 bpm):
   `Kick  X . . . | X . . . | X . . . | X . . . `
   `Snare . . . . | X . . . | . . . . | X . . . `
   `Hat   x x x x | x x x x | x x x x | x x x x ` (16th sequenced hats/noise)
   Guitar 8th-note palm-muted chugs locked to the grid; Fear Factory-style kick follows guitar 16th syncopations (cyber metal).
7. Bass: synth bass doubling guitar or 8th sequenced bass.
8. Form: verse/chorus with intro synth loop, breakdown to drums+synth, 4 min (convention).
9. Mix: mechanical, compressed, gated, heavy use of distortion on drums.
10. Neighbours: NDH = German, theatrical, simpler; cyber metal = EBM/aggrotech elements, more melodic riffs [industrial wiki]; trap metal draws from industrial [trap metal wiki].
11. FL steps: program everything quantized; loop a 1-bar guitar riff sample (or MIDI) across the pattern; add synth arp and noise FX; distort the drum bus. Mistakes: humanized/live drum feel (industrial wants machine precision), too many riff variations.

#### Card M. Trap metal and 2020s crossovers
1. Identity: early 2010s US underground (Ghostemane, Scarlxrd, City Morgue, Bones-adjacent; $uicideboy$ adjacent) [trap metal wiki; eMastered]. References: Ghostemane "Mercury: Retrograde" and "Flesh", Scarlxrd "Heart Attack", City Morgue "33rd Blakk Glass". By ear: trap drum programming with blown-out distorted 808s, screamed/aggressive rap, down-tuned guitar or guitar samples, industrial noise.
2. Tempo: 130-160 bpm half-time (felt 65-80), cross-ref the trap/rage cards in `research_notes/Music theory and beat making/genres_hiphop_urban_global.md` (rage 140-170 half-time) (convention).
3. Harmony: minor/Phrygian 1-2 note loops; tritone; harmonic minor guitar/synth hooks (convention).
4. Vocals: screamed rap, whispered ad-libs, heavy distortion on vocals.
5. Sounds: 808 drum machine, occasional guitar [trap metal wiki]; distorted/layered 808s [eMastered]; FL: FPC/808 kit + Fruity Blood Overdrive/Distructor or Fast Dist on 808, guitar loop or amp-simmed MIDI.
6. Rhythm (145 bpm half-time):
   `Kick  X . . . | . . . . | . . x . | . . . . `
   `Snare . . . . | . . . . | X . . . | . . . . `
   `Hat   x x x x | x x x x | x x x x | x . x x ` (rolls/triplets at bar ends)
   808 follows kick and guitar root, slides to b2.
7. Bass: distorted 808 (clipped) carrying the riff.
8. Form: rap form (intro 8 / hook 8 / verse 16 / hook / verse / hook) per earlier rap template; songs often 2-3 min (convention).
9. Mix: clipped, loud, distorted master bus.
10. Neighbours: rage (supersaw leads, less guitar), nu metal (live band), metalcore with trap breakdown drops (Bad Omens/Sleep Token-style hybrids).
11. FL steps: trap drum pattern in FPC; 808 through heavy distortion then low-pass and clipper; a drop-tuned palm-muted guitar loop on beats 1 and 3 of the half-time bar; scream vocal chain. Mistakes: clean polite 808 (sourced: distorted 808s define the sound [eMastered]), metal double-kick (wrong drum language).

## 2. What drum techniques define each style, written as grids?

### Takeaway
Six drum engines cover nearly all of metal: rock beat plus gallop (NWOBHM/power), skank/thrash beat (thrash, black/death mid-sections, d-beat for crust/sludge), double-kick 16ths under backbeat (power, melodeath, metalcore verses), blast beats (death, black, deathcore, grind; traditional, hammer, bomb, gravity), half-time breakdowns with kicks locked to guitar (metalcore, deathcore, slam), and polymetric kick-follows-guitar grooves over a 4/4 hand pattern (djent). Nu metal and trap metal borrow hip-hop/trap grids instead.

### Cited Findings
- Blast beat variants: traditional blast = single-stroke roll alternating snare and kick with the ride in unison with the kick; skank beat = sped-up 2/4 polka beat where the ride hits with both kick and snare, called the "Slayer"/"thrash" beat in the US; bomb blast = 16th-note kick under 8th-note snare (blast + double bass) — [Wikipedia: Blast beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_beat)
- Four main blast variants: traditional, bomb, hammer, freehand — [Wikipedia: Blast beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_beat). The hammer blast (kick, snare, cymbal all in unison) was reportedly invented by drummers who could not yet reach traditional-blast speeds — [electronicdrumadvisor](https://www.electronicdrumadvisor.com/?p=5858) (secondary source).
- Gallop: kick figure of one 8th plus two 16ths — [freedrumlessons/drumeo](https://freedrumlessons.com/drum-lessons/heavy-metal-drumming.php)
- D-beat is named after Discharge drummer Terry "Tezz" Roberts' beat; associated with crust punk and Swedish crust/death-metal-leaning bands (Wolfbrigade, Disfear); sludge drummers use d-beat in fast passages — [Wikipedia: D-beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-beat); [Wikipedia: Doom metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_metal)
- Djent: hands usually play a straight 4/4 groove while the feet follow the polymetric guitar pattern; riff cycles (for example 3/16 in "Bleed", or 19/16, 23/16 cycles) phase against 4/4 drums until they realign; groupings like 5-5-5-1, 6-6-4, 3-7-6 within 16 sixteenths, or 7-7-7-7-4 across 2 bars — [Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/articles/26211-obsessive-progressive-how-to-decode-advanced-polymeters); [search summary of djent forum discussion](https://tl.net/forum/general/120184-djent-will-be-huge)
- Nu metal drummers are hip-hop-influenced; blast beats and double bass uncommon — [Wikipedia: Nu metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nu_metal)

### Inferences — grid library (convention, built from the sourced definitions)

`Kick  x . x . | x . x . | x . x . | x . x . `
`Snare . x . x | . x . x | . x . x | . x . x `
`Cym   x . x . | x . x . | x . x . | x . x . `

`Snare x . x . | x . x . | x . x . | x . x . `
`Cym   x . x . | x . x . | x . x . | x . x . `

G3. Bomb blast (kick 16ths, snare 8ths) [sourced definition]:
`Kick  x x x x | x x x x | x x x x | x x x x `
`Snare x . x . | x . x . | x . x . | x . x . `
`Cym   x . x . | x . x . | x . x . | x . x . `

G5. Skank / thrash beat (180-220 bpm) — see Card B grid.

G6. D-beat (160-200 bpm; one common transcription, convention — verify by ear against Discharge "Hear Nothing See Nothing Say Nothing"):

## 3. How do breakdowns, song forms and arrangements work?

### Takeaway
Breakdowns are slow, palm-muted, low-string sections. They are usually half-time against the song bpm, with kicks in unison with the guitar rhythm and a china/crash on quarters. They are the arrangement climax in metalcore and deathcore and the mosh cue live. Song forms split into two camps: verse/chorus forms (classic heavy, power, symphonic, nu metal, metalcore) and riff-chain forms (thrash middle sections, death, black, doom, djent), where riffs repeat in cycles and tempo or feel changes create the sections.

### Cited Findings
- Breakdowns in deathcore/metalcore: slow-paced strumming or fast syncopated triplet-feel patterns, palm-muted on the lowest three strings, tunings from Drop D down; may include a bass drop; signal for moshing; sometimes criticized as overused — [Wikipedia: Breakdown (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakdown_(music))
- The hardcore breakdown developed in early hardcore (Bad Brains' reggae/hardcore mix); Integrity merged hardcore with metal solos and chugging riffs; Ringworm emphasized breakdowns; some acts fused Slayer-style riffs with Pantera-leaning breakdowns — [Wikipedia: Metalcore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metalcore)
- Metalcore breakdowns may use pick scrapes and dissonant chords; metalcore form = aggressive verses, melodic choruses, breakdowns — [Wikipedia: Metalcore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metalcore)
- Deathcore breakdown = single palm-muted notes repeated on low open strings — [Wikipedia: Deathcore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deathcore)
- Death metal uses abrupt tempo, key and meter changes; black metal uses unconventional structures and atmosphere; doom uses repetitive rhythms with little regard for harmonic progression and structure — [Death metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_metal); [Black metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_metal); [Doom metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_metal)
- Sleep Token's "The Summoning" moves from piano intro to R&B verse to a low-tuned metal breakdown and a jazz-tinged outro; Sleep Token's Even in Arcadia reached No. 1 on the Billboard 200 in 2025; Ghost's Skeleta also hit No. 1 in spring 2025; Lorna Shore, Spiritbox and Sleep Token tracks trended on TikTok in 2025 — [Cherwell](https://cherwell.org/2025/06/01/oxford-sleep-token-metal/); [Chartmetric/Hypebot](https://hmc.chartmetric.com/sleep-token-turnstile-hard-rock-bands-top-charts-2025/)
- Bad Omens' "Just Pretend" (2022) went viral on TikTok, making them one of the biggest modern metalcore acts — [Cherwell](https://cherwell.org/2025/06/01/oxford-sleep-token-metal/)

### Inferences
Breakdown recipe (convention, consistent with the sources)
1. Set up: cut everything for 1 beat to 1 bar (silence, a vocal call, or a pick scrape or riser) before the breakdown downbeat.
2. Feel: half-time (snare on beat 3 / step 9) at the same bpm, or a tempo drop (deathcore "final breakdown" often 10-30% slower via tempo automation).
3. Guitar: open lowest string 16th/8th chugs with deliberate 16th rests; optional b2 or tritone stabs (e.g., Drop C: C2 chugs + Db/Gb chord hits); triplet-feel groupings (3+3+3+3+4) for syncopated breakdowns [breakdown wiki].
4. Drums: kick in unison with every chug (G9), china/crash on quarters, toms fills between phrases; the last bar often goes quarter-note "stomps".
5. Low end: sub drop (sine or 808, 1-2 bars, pitch-falling) on the breakdown downbeat [bass drop: breakdown wiki].
6. Length: 8 or 16 bars; repeat 2x with variation (add octave guitar or dissonant lead the second time).

Form templates by genre (bars, convention)
- Classic/NWOBHM/power: Intro 8 / V 16 / Pre 8 / Ch 8 / V 16 / Pre 8 / Ch 8 / Solo 16-32 / Ch 16 (key change) / Outro 4-8.
- Thrash: Intro riff 8 / V 16 / Ch 8 / V 16 / Ch 8 / Middle: 2-4 new riffs, tempo change, 2-3 solos (32-64) / Recap V+Ch / Outro.
- Death/black: Riff A x4-8 / Riff B x4-8 / A / C (blast) / D (groove or slow) / A; black metal riffs repeat longer, songs 6-10 min.
- Doom: Riff A x8-16 / verse over A / Riff B / long solo or drone / A; 7-15 min.
- Nu metal: Intro 4-8 / V 16 (quiet, rapped) / Ch 8 (loud, sung) / V 16 / Ch 8 / Bridge-breakdown 8-16 / Ch 16.
- Metalcore: Intro 4-8 / V 16 / Pre 4-8 / Ch 8 / V 16 / Pre / Ch 8 / Bridge 8 (clean/ambient) / Breakdown 8-16 / Ch 8-16 / Outro; ~3:30.
- Deathcore: Intro / riff-blast 16 / breakdown 8 / riff 16 / breakdown 8 / bridge (orchestral or slam) / final slowed breakdown 8-16.
- Djent: Riff cycle A (16-32) / clean ambient section / Riff B / climax riff over vocal / ambient outro.
- Hybrid 2025 style (Sleep Token, Bad Omens): genre-shifting sections inside one song (piano/R&B verse into djent/metalcore breakdown), electronic production and pop hooks.

Arrangement habits: guitars L/R play the same riff (quad-tracked); leads in the centre or slightly panned; bass and kick lock to the guitar rhythm; dynamics come from stops, half-time switches and instrumentation drop-outs rather than volume automation (convention).

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of metal song-section bar counts was found; templates above are practitioner conventions.
- 2025-2026 trend data rely on student/press and chart-analytics articles. I found no authoritative industry analysis of "nu-metalcore" or trap-metal production trends.

## 4. How do producers emulate metal guitars, bass and drums in a DAW?

### Takeaway
Modern metal production is a "built" sound. Guitars are quad-tracked (or double-tracked) through a boost pedal into an amp sim and IR, with high-pass around 100-150 Hz, low-pass around 10-12 kHz and mud cuts at 250-500 Hz. Bass is usually split into a clean sub band plus a distorted mid/top band. Drums are sample-reinforced or fully replaced: the kick is layered from click/beater, body and sub components and compressed hard. For a composer-AI working from MIDI, the biggest realism levers are short staccato palm-mute note lengths, real double-tracking (two different performances or humanized copies), velocity variation on fast kicks, and a tight, high-passed low end.

### Cited Findings
Guitars
- Quad tracking: pan pair 1 at 100% L/R, pair 2 at 70-80% L/R; make pair 2 slightly different (less gain, different boost model, different IR or pickup) — [Nail The Mix: quad tracking](https://www.nailthemix.com/quad-tracking-guitars)
- Guitar bus: slow attack, fast release compression; carve 300-500 Hz for snare and vocals; add high-end air — [Nail The Mix: quad tracking](https://www.nailthemix.com/quad-tracking-guitars)
- HPF guitars at 100-120 Hz or higher (100-150 Hz for low tunings); LPF starting around 10-12 kHz to tame fizz; mud/boxiness 250-500 Hz (narrow Q cuts); VCA bus compression 1-2 dB GR; multiband compression on 100-250 Hz "woof" to control palm mutes; use less gain than you think; layering beats single high-gain tracks; amp sims such as Neural DSP, STL Tones, Positive Grid are viable — [Nail The Mix: guitar mixing FAQs](https://www.nailthemix.com/guitar-mixing-faqs)
- Tube Screamer-style boost before the amp: drive low, level high, tone up, to tighten lows and focus attack; then reduce amp gain so chugs stay percussive — [search summary of Nail The Mix / Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-get-a-metal-guitar-tone/)

Drums
- Sample replacement/blending is practically standard in modern metal for consistency and punch; tools: Slate Trigger 2, Drumagog 5, DAW samplers; deathcore/technical metal often 100% replaces kick and snare — [Nail The Mix: drum mixing FAQs](https://www.nailthemix.com/drum-mixing-faqs)
- Kick layering: click/beater layer high-passed hard (100-300 Hz); body layer boosted 60-100 Hz; sub layer = sine at 40-60 Hz low-passed above 80-100 Hz. EQ: HPF 30-40 Hz at 18-24 dB/oct; cut boxiness 200-500 Hz; +2-4 dB at 60-100 Hz; beater/click boost 2-8 kHz; optional 200-300 Hz dip for snare space — [Nail The Mix: modern metal kick](https://nailthemix.com/kick-drum)
- Kick compression: FET attack 5-10 ms, release 50-150 ms, ratio 4:1-8:1, 3-6 dB GR; parallel crush at 10-20 dB GR blended under; multiband sidechain ducks bass below 150-200 Hz on kick hits — [Nail The Mix: modern metal kick](https://nailthemix.com/kick-drum)
- Kick-bass: find the kick fundamental (e.g., 60 Hz) and dip the bass there, boosting bass at 80-100 Hz or 40-50 Hz instead — [Nail The Mix: modern metal kick](https://nailthemix.com/kick-drum)
- Kick sample blending also involves phase alignment, EQ and multiband compression (Buster Odeholm session) — [Nail The Mix](https://nailthemix.com/allt-kick-drum-buster-odeholm-samples)

Genre-specific production
- Black metal's raw, lo-fi production with hiss is deliberate; trebly guitars — [Black metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_metal)
- Trap metal: heavily distorted 808s; producers layer multiple 808s with different attack/release/distortion — [eMastered](https://emastered.com/es/blog/what-is-dark-trap-music)
- Industrial: drum machines, samplers, processed vocals — [Industrial metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_metal)
- Symphonic: workstation presets or sequenced virtual orchestras are accepted practice — [Symphonic metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphonic_metal)

### Inferences — FL Studio implementation (convention unless linked)
Signal chains
- Guitar from MIDI: use a dedicated metal-guitar instrument (Shreddage 3, Ample Metal-class) that outputs DI, then a boost (TS-style) into an amp sim and cab IR. FL stock fallback: "Hardcore" (FL's guitar multi-FX with amp/cab stages) or Distructor's amp+cab modules after a DI-like source. Expect stock-only to sound synthetic.
- Guitar bus: HPF 100-150 Hz, LPF 10-12 kHz, narrow cuts 250-500 Hz, gentle bus comp (1-2 dB), multiband/dynamic EQ on 100-250 Hz for palm-mute thump [NTM numbers]. Fruity parametric EQ 2 and Maximus/Multiband can do this in stock FL.
- Bass: split at ~100-250 Hz: clean low band (compressed) + distorted upper band (Fast Dist/Blood Overdrive/Distructor) with cab sim; HPF ~30-40 Hz; sidechain low band to kick. The split frequency is convention, not sourced.
- Drums: FPC or a drum ROMpler (Superior Drummer / GetGood / Steven Slate Drums free / MT Power Drum Kit free). Layer the kick per NTM: click + body + sine sub (FL: drop a short sine from 3x Osc or BooBass-free sine under the kick). Snare: layer a sample, room/plate reverb; china and ride on separate mixer tracks.
- Panning: rhythm guitars 100/100 (pair 1) and 70-80 (pair 2) [NTM]; bass, kick, snare, lead vocal centre; leads slightly off-centre; toms panned as the drummer sees them.
- Loudness: modern metalcore/deathcore masters are very dense; trap metal deliberately clipped; black/doom/stoner less limited (convention; not sourced).

Making MIDI guitars convincing
1. Palm mutes = short notes (about 1/32 to 1/16 length at 150 bpm) with velocities 90-110 and random +-5; open chords = full length (convention).
2. Double tracking: never duplicate the same MIDI L/R. Write two takes, or copy and humanize timing (+-5-10 ms) and velocities, and use a different instrument/amp/IR per side [quad tracking differences: NTM].
3. Power-chord voicing: root + 5th (+ octave) on low strings; avoid 3rds under heavy gain (mud); put 3rds and extensions in clean or lead layers.
4. Pitch reality: keep riff roots within the instrument's range (Drop C lowest = C2/MIDI 36; 7-string B1 = 35; 8-string F#1 = 30) (derived).
5. Add noises: pick scrapes, slides, string squeals, feedback swells at section changes.
6. Tighten everything to the grid for modern genres (djent, deathcore, metalcore, industrial). Leave old-school/doom/stoner/black looser.

Common "fake" giveaways (cross-genre): flat-velocity machine-gun kicks; long legato palm mutes; single guitar centre-panned; unfiltered sub-bass from guitars masking the kick; drums with no room/overheads; no silence before breakdowns; wrong drum engine for the genre (blasts in nu metal, trap hats in thrash).

How to tell the subgenres apart by ear (quick key)
- Gallop + twin leads + high clean vocal, E standard = NWOBHM/trad. Same plus constant double-kick, major-ish anthems = power. Plus dominant orchestra/soprano = symphonic.
- Fast palm-muted chromatic riffs + skank beat + shouts = thrash. Lower, growled, blasts, chromatic clusters = death. Un-muted tremolo chords + shrieks + trebly lo-fi = black.
- Very slow + fuzz + tritone = doom; plus screams, feedback, hardcore bursts = sludge; groovier, psychedelic, melodic vocals = stoner.
- Hip-hop beat + 7-string single-note syncopation + rap/scream, no solos = nu metal.
- Screamed verse + clean chorus + half-time breakdown = metalcore; much lower, growls/squeals, blasts + open-string breakdowns = deathcore.
- Odd-grouped chug cycles over a steady 4/4 snare, extended range = djent.
- Machine-tight drum machine, looped riffs, synths = industrial. Trap hats + distorted 808 + screamed rap = trap metal.
