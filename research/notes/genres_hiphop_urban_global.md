# Genre Recipes: Hip Hop, Urban and Global Beat Styles (for an FL Studio beat-making skill)

Notation used in all grids (one bar = 16 sixteenth-note steps, beats fall on steps 1, 5, 9, 13):
`X` = accent (vel ~100-127), `x` = normal hit (vel ~75-100), `g` = ghost (vel ~35-60), `r` = roll start (1/32 or 1/16-triplet burst), `.` = rest. Steps shown in groups of four: `1234 5678 9-12 13-16`.
Source-quality note: Wikipedia, Native Instruments, Splice, Attack Magazine, Roland and The FADER are the strongest sources used. beatstorapon.com, audeobox.com, orphiq.com, violetrecording.com, musicproductionwiki.com and tunee.ai are tutorial/aggregator sites of lower authority; their numbers are consistent with each other and with the stronger sources, but treat them as practitioner convention rather than canon.

## Q1. What defines each genre's rhythm and sound, and how do close relatives differ?

### Takeaway
Each genre is identified mostly by its drum grammar plus one signature low-end/lead element: trap = half-time snare on beat 3 + rolling hats + long 808s; drill = 140ish BPM with 3+3+2 skippy hats, a snare that moves to beat 4 in the second bar, and sliding 808s; plugg/pluggnb = trap tempo with soft sine/bell leads and jazzy electric-piano block chords; rage = trap rhythm under short, looped, stereo-wide future-bass synth leads and distorted 808s; Afrobeats = interlocking percussion (16th shakers, 3-2 clave, congas) at ~100-115 BPM; amapiano = four-on-the-floor house pulse at ~108-115 BPM with a pitched "log drum" bass; reggaeton = the 3+3+2 dembow snare over a kick pulse at ~85-100 BPM; phonk = 808 cowbell melodies, Memphis vocal chops and distorted 808s; Jersey club = the five-kick (1,5,9,12,15) run at 130-140 BPM with chopped vocals and the bed-squeak.

### Cited Findings

**Trap**
- Trap's defining sound: deep 808 kick drums, double/triple-time hi-hats, crisp (often layered) snares, synthesized strings and brass; typical beats are felt at ~70 BPM but programmed in half-time at 140 BPM — [Wikipedia: Trap music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trap_music)
- Foundational producers: Shawty Redd (minimal, horror-tinged), DJ Toomp (melodic, sample-based); Lex Luger popularised the modern template (heavy 808s, crisp snares, orchestration) and produced 200+ songs in 2010-2011; later Metro Boomin, Southside (808 Mafia), Young Chop — [Wikipedia: Trap music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trap_music)
- NI's trap tutorial works at 140 BPM and notes trap tempos ranging 130-200 BPM; claps on beat 3 of bars, 8th-note closed hats with the last hats turned into 32nd-note rolls (three notes per roll, middle notes lower velocity); snare fill of straight 8ths in the last bar of a 4-bar loop; clap envelope sustain at zero, decay ~280 ms; key C minor with counter-melody built from root, octave, fifth and minor seventh — [Native Instruments: How to make a trap beat](https://blog.native-instruments.com/how-to-make-a-trap-beat/)
- In trap the snare/clap plays a straighter part than the hats, heard on beat 3 of the bar; 808s are long-sustain pitched sub notes that often slide; hats use triplet rolls, open-hat accents and rapid 32nds — [Native Instruments trap guide](https://blog.native-instruments.com/how-to-make-a-trap-beat/) (via search summary); [TIDAL: What makes trap trap](https://tidal.com/magazine/article/what-makes-trap-trap/1-72722)

**Drill (UK, NY/Brooklyn, Chicago)**
- UK drill: 138-151 BPM, faster than Chicago; hi-hats follow a steady 3+3+2 polyrhythm instead of trap's on-beat pattern; the snare lands on the fourth beat instead of the third every two bars; sliding bass with hard kicks; dark grime-influenced melodies; emerged in early-2010s Brixton from Chicago drill + UK road rap + grime — [Wikipedia: UK drill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_drill)
- UK drill producers: Carns Hill (early 67 beats), QUIETPVCK, Mazza & GottiOnEm; AXL Beats, 808Melo and Ghosty shaped Brooklyn drill's adoption — [Wikipedia: UK drill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_drill)
- Pop Smoke's "Welcome to the Party" and "Dior" were produced by UK beatmakers 808Melo and AXL Beats: Brooklyn drill = UK-style sliding 808s and drum patterns under a New York vocal cadence — [orphiq: Drill music](https://orphiq.com/resources/what-is-drill-music) (via search summary)
- Drill tempos: UK/NY 140-150 BPM on a fast grid with slow half-time feel; Chicago drill often 60-75 BPM (or programmed double-time); sample drill 130-145. Snare: bar 1 main snare on beat 3, bar 2 on beat 4 ("lurching two-bar motion"). Hats: start from 8ths, delete hits to leave gaps, add 16th/triplet bursts before main snares, vary velocities. Kicks are not placed under every 808 note; used as pickups/phrase accents — [beatstorapon: How to make a drill beat](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/how-to-make-a-drill-beat/)
- Regional lanes: UK = broken two-bar pocket, syncopated hats, restless kicks, longer controlled 808 slides and cold motifs; NY/Brooklyn = harder impact, direct momentum, hook-ready repetition, heavy tuned slides and punchy drums; Chicago = heavy, direct, closer to trap, fewer long slides; Afro-drill = drill tension with more fluid layered percussion — [beatstorapon](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/how-to-make-a-drill-beat/)
- In Chicago drill the 808s are sustained and fairly static; in UK and Brooklyn drill the 808s slide between pitches and carry melodic weight; NY drill harmony tends toward simpler minor triads with the sliding 808 carrying harmony — [orphiq: Drill chords UK vs NY](https://orphiq.com/resources/drill-chords); [orphiq: Trap vs drill](https://orphiq.com/resources/trap-vs-drill-comparison) (via search summary)
- UK drill working tempo 138-145 (up to ~151), half-time feel; hats are "skippy", hand-programmed with deliberate gaps rather than pasted 16th rolls; production philosophy is restraint — dark, minimal, let the 808 talk — [Soundtrap: UK drill](https://blog.soundtrap.com/?p=734); [Unison: drill beats](https://unison.audio/how-to-make-drill-beats/) (via search summary)

**Plugg / pluggnb**
- Plugg beats sit ~130-150 BPM, usually with a half-time feel (~65-75 BPM perceived) while hats move at the faster tempo; many tutorials start at 130 BPM; a simple warm 2-3 chord minor progression sets the mood — [Reason Studios: How to make plugg beats](https://www.reasonstudios.com/news/post/how-to-make-plugg-beats) (via search summary)
- Pluggnb = plugg beats + R&B sensibility; harmonic backbone is block chords on electric piano with minor 7ths and added 9ths and "experimental inversions" that put tensions right next to the root; instruments: electric keys, sine-wave synth leads, strings, glassy synth bells, 808s with plugg drum kits, re-pitched gospel vocal samples as ear candy; vocal harmony influenced by 90s R&B groups (Jodeci, Dru Hill); producers MexikoDro and StoopidXool credited with plugg's emergence; pluggnb artists Summrs, Autumn!, XanGang, ARIZONATEARS, wintfye — [Splice: What is pluggnb](https://splice.com/blog/what-is-pluggnb/)
- Example pluggnb progression: Fmaj9 – Cmaj9/B – Dm9 – Cmaj9/E; electric piano with subtle delay recommended — appeared in search snippet attributed to the pluggnb guides from [Native Instruments](https://blog.native-instruments.com/pluggnb-music/) / [SampleFocus](https://samplefocus.com/blog/make-pluggnb-beats-samples/) (not verified by direct fetch)

**Rage**
- Rage is a trap subgenre (late 2010s US); the name comes from Trippie Redd & Playboi Carti's 2021 "Miss the Rage"; early sound = short, looping, stereo-widened, future-bass-influenced synth lead hooks over basic energetic trap rhythms and heavy 808s, often reminiscent of 80s-90s video-game music and frequently sourced from royalty-free EDM sample packs (e.g., Cymatics); key producers F1lthy, Pi'erre Bourne, Maaly Raw; artists Playboi Carti, Lil Uzi Vert, Trippie Redd, Yeat, SoFaygo, Opium (Ken Carson, Destroy Lonely); 2023-2025 moved toward more extreme, distorted, bass-heavy production with screamed auto-tuned vocals (OsamaSon, Che, Nettspend); overlaps with plugg, hyperpop and UK underground — [Wikipedia: Rage rap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rage_rap)
- Whole Lotta Red (2020) primarily produced by F1lthy and Art Dealer, with Pi'erre Bourne, Maaly Raw, Lil 88, Jasper Harris, Ojivolta, Wheezy — [Wikipedia: Whole Lotta Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_Lotta_Red) (via search summary)
- Rage tempos: 140-160 BPM, or 150-170 in half-time; minor key; distorted 808 pitched to the melody then driven/clipped; three lanes — raw rage, melodic-ethereal, plugg-sample — [musicproductionwiki: rage beats](https://musicproductionwiki.com/articles/how-to-make-rage-beats) (low-authority source); example rage synth loops posted at 154 and 155 BPM — [Looperman loop 368114](https://www.looperman.com/loops/detail/368114/rage-synth-and-lead-trippie-redd-x-playboi-carti-free-154bpm-trap-synth-loop); [Looperman loop 321932](https://www.looperman.com/loops/detail/321932/trippie-redd-x-playboi-carti-i-luv-rage-lead-free-155bpm-hip-hop-synth-loop)

**Boom bap**
- Boom bap: 85-95 BPM, swing-heavy MPC-style drums, chopped vinyl jazz/soul/funk samples; "boom" = deep round kick, "bap" = tight snapping snare; MPC 60 (1988) was DJ Premier's favourite until the MPC 3000 (1994) — [violetrecording: What is boom bap](https://violetrecording.com/what-is-boom-bap/) (via search summary)
- Attack Magazine's 90s boom bap dissection: two layered kick samples balanced with velocity variation; clap nudged a few ms late; 16th hats with a cowbell on beat 1 of bar 2 and an open hat on beat 4 of bar 2; swing MPC 3000 8ths 57% on kick/snare, MPC 3000 16ths 74% on hats; kick EQ small boost ~60 Hz, big dip ~150 Hz; snare/clap into reverb with sidechain from the kick; hats auto-panned; sample (Cornelius Brothers & Sister Rose "Since I Found My Baby", -5 semitones) low-cut and slightly darkened — [Attack Magazine: 90s boom bap](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/90s-boom-bap-hip-hop/)

**Lo-fi hip hop**
- Lo-fi generally 60-90 BPM (tutorial at 65 BPM); example looping VI–i–V7 (A minor: F – Am – E7) with extended tensions; soft thumpy kick, loose snare, acoustic hats, intentionally not fully quantised; textures: vinyl emulation, bitcrushing, low-pass from ~3.12 kHz with gentle slope, high-pass at 20 Hz, reverb/delay; elements introduced one at a time over 4- or 8-bar phrases with reverse-crash transitions — [Native Instruments: Lo-fi hip hop](https://blog.native-instruments.com/lo-fi-hip-hop/)
- Lo-fi harmony: maj7, m7, dom7 plus occasional 9ths/11ths; ii–V–I (Dm7–G7–Cmaj7) as a starting point; vinyl crackle, tape hiss, tape wobble, filtering, saturation — [Mode Audio: Lo-fi essentials](https://modeaudio.com/magazine/lofi-hip-hop-5-production-essentials); [Unison: making lofi beats](https://unison.audio/making-lofi-beats/) (via search summary)

**Modern R&B**
- Modern R&B mostly 60-90 BPM; trap-influenced R&B often programmed at 120-140 BPM in half-time; neo-soul ~68-95; built on 7th/9th (also 11th/13th) chords, 808 or live bass tuned to chord roots, swung drums with ghost notes, Rhodes and pads, pitched vocal chops, spacious reverb, vocals front and centre — [violetrecording: R&B beats](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-make-rnb-beats/); [musicproductionwiki: R&B](https://musicproductionwiki.com/articles/how-to-make-rnb-music) (via search summary)

**Afrobeats**
- Groove is built from layered, interlocking percussion rather than the Western kick/snare backbone: syncopated kicks, rim/clap backbeat, continuous 16th shakers (swung), congas/bongos/woodblock, sometimes a pitched log-drum bass that answers the kick — [violetrecording: Afrobeats](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-make-afrobeats/) (via search summary)
- Afrobeats tempo 95-115 BPM, 105-110 most common, 108 "sweet spot"; kick on beat 1, "and" of 2, beat 3 (75-85% vel), "and" of 4 on alternating bars; snare/clap 2 & 4 with a 40% ghost on the "e" of 4 every other bar; closed hats straight 8ths (70-80% downbeats, 50-60% upbeats), open hat on "and" of 2 with cut-group/choke; shaker 16ths with velocity cycle 60-40-70-40%; 3-2 clave/woodblock on 1, and-of-1, and-of-2, 4, and-of-4 (80-90%); congas accents 90%, ghosts 35-50%; rim fills gaps; keys typically C/G/F/D major with simple triads; muted staccato guitar 8ths/16ths (accents 100%, mutes 50-65%) with 2-3 note runs; warm round bass C1-E2 on chord roots; arrangement intro 4-8, verse 16, hook 8, bridge 8 bars — [audeobox: Afrobeats in FL Studio](https://www.audeobox.com/learn/fl-studio/how-to-make-afrobeats-in-fl-studio/)
- Afrobeats median ~110 BPM — [mixgraph: BPM for Afrobeats](https://www.mixgraph.io/bpm-for/afrobeats) (via search summary)

**Amapiano**
- Amapiano 110-115 BPM (slower than typical house, faster than deep house); "without the log drum, it's not amapiano"; timekeeper element = offbeat element playing quarter notes; jazzy chords, smooth piano; log drum design: layer a sub with identical ADSR, keep phase-aligned, don't cut the log drum's lows, drive via Fruity WaveShaper or KNOCK, soft clipper for drive; Fruity DX10 contains the original log-drum preset; influences deep house, jazz, gqom, lounge; references Vigro Deep, Muziqal Tone, De Mthuda, Surreal Sessions (Kooldrink interview) — [Splice: What is amapiano](https://splice.com/blog/what-is-amapiano-music/)
- Roland: amapiano 108-115 BPM; example 1 = four-on-the-floor kick, constant 16th shaker, open hat on the offbeats (third 16th of each beat), conga on beat 2, "a" of 2, "and" of 3, and 4; a 909 kick on every quarter; example 2 uses a more syncopated kick; log drum built by layering a synth kick + sine + marimba/synth bass + kalimba with pitch modulation; chords Cm9 – F7 – Bbmaj9 – Gm7 – G7b9 (jazz ii-V-I logic) — [Roland Articles: Production hacks, Amapiano](https://articles.roland.com/?p=67237)
- Log drum = pitched percussive bass with a fast pitch glide ("rubbery" bounce), syncopated, bouncing between kicks rather than landing on them; 112 BPM practical start; two-bar log-drum phrase (bar 1 states, bar 2 answers); untuned log drum destabilises the track; example chords Em9 – Cmaj7 – Gmaj7 – D6/9, no mandatory key; arrangement intro 8-16, foundation 16, development 16-32, breakdown 8-16, outro 8-16 bars — [beatstorapon: Amapiano](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/how-to-make-amapiano-beat/)

**Reggaeton / dembow**
- Dembow beat named after Shabba Ranks' 1990 "Dem Bow"; rooted in Steely & Clevie's 1989 "Fish Market"/"Pocoman Jam" riddim; Dennis the Menace's "Pounder (Dub Mix II)" became the heavily sampled Puerto Rican source; "boom-ch-boom-chick" with a 3+3+2 tresillo cross-rhythm; Dominican dembow is a separate style — [Wikipedia: Dembow beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dembow_beat)
- Reggaeton foundation: kick on beats 1 and 3 (commonly a full four-on-the-floor), with the snare on the last 16th of beats 1 and 3 and halfway through beats 2 and 4 — i.e., gaps of 3, 3 and 2 sixteenths ("pop triplet") — [FaderPro: 5 kick and snare patterns](https://blog.faderpro.com/techniques/5-kick-and-snare-patterns-you-need-to-know/) (via search summary); round dull kick for the four-on-the-floor pulse — [UJAM: How to make a dembow groove](https://www.ujam.com/tutorials/how-to-make-a-dembow-groove/) (via search summary)
- Reggaeton 85-100 BPM (modern radio ~92-96); Dominican dembow 115-130 BPM, drum-forward, heavier/distorted bass, loop-based harmony, rapid chant vocals; Dominican references El Alfa, Tokischa, Rochy RD, Kiko El Crazy — [orphiq: What is dembow](https://orphiq.com/resources/what-is-dembow); [orphiq: What is reggaeton](https://orphiq.com/resources/what-is-reggaeton) (via search summary)
- Reggaeton bass = sustained synth sub or 808 under the dembow, simple root-following lines; Latin percussion (congas, timbales, shakers); heavy sidechain pumping — [orphiq: What is reggaeton](https://orphiq.com/resources/what-is-reggaeton) (via search summary)

**Phonk (Memphis/classic, drift, Brazilian)**
- Phonk: early-2010s US hip-hop subgenre; Memphis-rap vocals, chopped-and-screwed techniques, 90s hip-hop samples with jazz/funk; term coined by SpaceGhostPurrp ("phonk" = funk, nod to G-funk); roots in DJ Screw and Three 6 Mafia; early artists Denzel Curry, Xavier Wulf, Lil Ugly Mane, DJ Smokey; TR-808 with prominent cowbell, distorted heavy bass; drift phonk from late-2010s Russia with faster tempos and aggressive bass (Kordhell "Murder in My Mind"); Brazilian funk / funk automotivo associated with phonk by mid-2020s ("Brazilian phonk"); short-form video soundtrack — [Wikipedia: Phonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonk)
- Drift phonk 130-160 BPM with aggressive cowbell patterns and distorted 808s; Brazilian phonk ~130-150 with funk carioca rhythms and MC chants — [tunee.ai phonk](https://www.tunee.ai/music-generator/phonk) (low-authority AI-tool page; treat as approximate)
- Funk mandelão is made at 130 or 140 BPM with synths (São Paulo baile scene, e.g., Baile da DZ7) — [ABCdoABC: Brazilian phonk](https://abcdoabc.com.br/brazilian-phonk-por-que-o-funk-brasileiro-ganhou-outro-nome-e-se-espalhou-pela-europa/) (via search summary); funk automotivo/mandelão often ~150 BPM with distorted bass and industrial minimalism; 130 BPM tamborzão common — [Suno: Brazilian funk genre page](https://suno.com/music-library/genres/brazilian-funk) (via search summary; low authority)

**Jersey club**
- Jersey club: Newark, NJ; pioneered by DJ Tameil (Brick Bandits); derives from Baltimore club; NI example at 142 BPM, typical 130-140; kick on 16th steps 1, 5, 9, 12, 15 ("two quarter notes, two dotted eighths, one eighth"); snare on 9 and 12 (bars 1 & 3) and 9, 11, 12 (bars 2 & 4) with velocity variation; closed hats on beats 2 and 4; crash on beat 1 marking sections; elements layer in every 8 bars, lead/vocal drops at bar 41, returns at 49, then progressive removal — [Native Instruments: Jersey club](https://blog.native-instruments.com/jersey-club/)
- Five-kick skeleton `X--- X--- X--X --X-`; bed-squeak sample from Trillville's "Some Cut"; vocal chops played like drums (single syllables at phrase ends, repeated words as hooks); short bass/808 envelopes so they don't overlap next kick, mono sub; DJ-friendly 8-bar blocks; for rap versions thin chops in verses and restore at hooks; 138-140 BPM practical start — [beatstorapon: Jersey club](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/how-to-make-a-jersey-club-beat/); [orphiq: Jersey club](https://orphiq.com/resources/what-is-jersey-club) (via search summary)

**West Coast (ratchet) and Detroit**
- DJ Mustard's "ratchet music" (early-mid 2010s): sparse but sometimes spastic, haunting synths, thumping 808 kicks, flurries of claps, a reverberated repeated "hey!" chant, producer tag; much of it Oakland/Bay-influenced (808s inspired by Bay Area artists) — [Wikipedia: Mustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_(music_producer)); [The FADER: Beat Construction, DJ Mustard](https://www.thefader.com/2012/8/20/beat-construction-dj-mustard)
- Detroit: producer Helluva is the architect of the current street sound ("Detroit trap"); east-side Detroit beats are piano-driven ("murderous piano beats") with DNA similar to West Coast gangsta rap; production leans into off-beat flows (Sada Baby) — [Okayplayer: Helluva interview](https://www.okayplayer.com/originals/helluva-interview-sada-baby-tee-grizzly-first-day-out-producer.html); [Okayplayer: Sada Baby](https://www.okayplayer.com/originals/sada-baby-interview-detroit-skuba-sada-2.html); [Metro Times: Helluva](https://www.metrotimes.com/music/for-detroit-producer-helluva-making-beats-is-a-tale-of-redemption-17406497) (via search summary)
- Detroit-style piano loops are posted at very fast tempos (example: 187 BPM "Sada Baby x Tee Grizzley x 42 Dugg" piano loop) — [Looperman loop 221153](https://www.looperman.com/loops/detail/221153/strapped-sada-baby-x-tee-grizzley-x-42-dugg-by-therealadrianr-free-187bpm-rap-piano-loop)

### Inferences

**Distinguishing close relatives (synthesised from the findings above)**
- Trap vs drill: both ~140 BPM half-time. Trap = snare/clap on beat 3 every bar, hats on a straight 8th/16th grid with rolls, 808 mostly sustained (slides optional). Drill = snare on beat 3 in bar 1 but beat 4 (plus pickup snares) in bar 2, hats grouped 3+3+2 with gaps, 808 slides are essential and carry the melody, kicks sparse/off-grid.
- UK vs NY drill: same skeleton. UK = colder, more syncopated, more melodic motifs, longer controlled glides, often 142-145. NY/Brooklyn = punchier, louder and warmer, heavier 808 glides, simpler minor-triad loops, hook-forward repetition. Chicago = trap-like, 808s held, ~60-75 BPM feel.
- Plugg vs pluggnb vs trap: plugg uses trap drums but swaps dark leads for soft sine/square leads, bells and lush pads; pluggnb adds electric-piano block chords with m7/add9 tensions and gospel/R&B vocal samples. Trap leads are darker (harmonic minor/phrygian bells, strings, brass).
- Rage vs trap/plugg: rage keeps simple trap drums but the lead is a short, looping, stereo-wide supersaw/future-bass synth and the 808 is distorted/clipped; energy and loudness are the point.
- Afrobeats vs amapiano: Afrobeats ~100-115 BPM with a syncopated, non-four-on-floor kick, 3-2 clave and guitars/plucks, song-length (~3 min) pop structure. Amapiano ~108-115 with a four-on-the-floor-derived house pulse (often sparse), log-drum bass as the hook, long jazzy piano/pad chords, shaker-heavy percussion and long DJ-style builds/breakdowns.
- Reggaeton vs Dominican dembow vs Afrobeats: all use 3+3+2. Reggaeton ~88-100, dembow snare over 4-floor kick; Dominican dembow is ~115-130, drum-forward, distorted bass.

**Recipe cards (grids are my derivations from the cited descriptions plus common practice; adjust to taste)**

**TRAP** — 130-150 BPM (default 140, half-time feel 70). Keys: minor, often harmonic minor / phrygian flavour (C#m, Fm, Gm common). Harmony: 2-4 chord minor loops, i–VI–VII, i–iv, half-step b2 tension. Sounds: bells, plucks, dark piano, orchestral strings/brass, choirs, flutes.
```
Kick      X... .... ..x. ....   (vary per bar; double kicks before snare common)
Snare/Clp .... .... X... ....   (beat 3 only; layered clap+snare)
Hat (cl)  x.x. x.x. x.x. x.rr   (8ths; 1/32 or 1/16T rolls into bar ends; vel ramps)
Open hat  .... .... .... ..x.   (occasional, step 15 or 7)
Perc      sparse: rim/snap on step 13 or 16, reversed crash into sections
```
808: long sustain, follows kick, glides of 1-12 semitones (often octave jumps) via FL slide notes / mono + portamento; clipped/saturated for phone audibility. Arrangement: 4-8 bar intro, 16-bar verse, 8-bar hook (repeat), 8-bar bridge, outro. Mix: 808 + kick loudest, bright hats, dark wide melody. References: Metro Boomin, Southside/808 Mafia, Lex Luger, Zaytoven.

**UK DRILL** — 138-151 (default 142-144). Keys: minor, phrygian b2 colour, harmonic minor. Melody: 2-4 note cold motifs on piano, music-box/bell plucks, strings, choirs, filtered pads; leave a gap every bar.
```
            Bar 1                    Bar 2
Kick      X... .... ..x. ....    X... ..x. .... ..x.
Snare     .... .... X... ....    .... .... .... X...   (+ optional ghost/pickup at step 8 or 11 of bar 2)
Hat       x..x ..x. x..x ..x.    x..x ..xr x..x .x..   (3+3+2 grouping, hand-edited gaps, triplet bursts before snares)
Perc      rim/snap fills, risers, reversed FX
```
808: sliding, monophonic, root -> 5th/octave slides near bar ends; glides are the melody. References: M1onthebeat, Carns Hill, Ghosty, AXL Beats, 808Melo.

**NY/BROOKLYN DRILL** — 140-145. Same grid as UK; harder layered snares/claps, heavier long 808 glides, simpler minor-triad loops, choir/voice samples; often vocal-sample (sample drill 130-145). References: Pop Smoke (808Melo, AXL Beats), Fivio Foreign, Sheff G.

**PLUGG / PLUGGNB** — 130-150 (half-time; default 140-150 for plugg, ~130-140 for pluggnb). Keys: plugg minor or major-ish dreamy; pluggnb major-7/minor-9 colour. Harmony: 2-3 chord loops; maj9/m9/add9, close inversions. Sounds: sine/square leads, glassy bells, Rhodes/electric piano, strings, gospel vocal chops.
```
Kick      X... .... ..x. ..x.
Snare/Clp .... .... X... ....   (often a soft clap/snap)
Hat       x.x. x.x. x.x. x.x.   (simpler than trap; occasional triplet roll)
Open hat  .... ..x. .... ....
```
808: clean, often sine-like, fewer extreme glides, short slides into chord changes. References: MexikoDro, StoopidXool, Summrs, Autumn!

**RAGE** — 140-170 half-time (loops cluster ~150-160). Minor keys. Sounds: short looping stereo-wide supersaw/future-bass synth hooks, video-game-style arps, distorted 808s.
```
Kick      X... .... ..X. ....   (often doubled with 808 hits)
Snare/Clp .... .... X... ....
Hat       x.x. x.x. x.x. x.x.   (simple, sometimes 16ths, occasional rolls)
```
808: distorted/clipped, pitched to melody, long. Mix: loud, saturated, wide lead, aggressive limiting. References: F1lthy, Art Dealer, Pi'erre Bourne; Playboi Carti "Whole Lotta Red", Ken Carson, Destroy Lonely, Yeat.

**BOOM BAP** — 85-95 (classic 88-94). Keys: whatever the sample is in (minor/dorian jazz-soul common). Harmony: chopped sample loops, minor 7/9 jazz chords. Sounds: chopped vinyl soul/jazz, horns, Rhodes, upright/electric bass, scratches.
```
Kick      X... ...x ..x. ....   (classic: 1, 8, 11; vary)
Snare     .... X... .... X...   (2 & 4; layered with clap nudged late)
Hat       x.g. x.g. x.g. x.g.   (16ths or 8ths with ghosts)
Open hat  .... .... .... ..x.   (bar 2 beat 4 per Attack)
Perc      cowbell/shaker accents (bar 2 beat 1 per Attack)
```
Swing: MPC 54-62% (Attack: 57% 8ths on kick/snare, 74% 16ths on hats). FL: channel swing ~15-35% and Groove/nudge. Bass: simple roots doubled with kick. References: DJ Premier, Pete Rock, J Dilla.

**LO-FI HIP HOP** — 60-90 (default 70-85). Keys: any, often minor/dorian; harmony ii–V–I, VI–i–V7, maj7/m7/9/11. Sounds: Rhodes/electric piano, nylon or clean guitar, soft pads, vinyl/tape texture.
```
Kick      X... ..x. ..x. ....
Snare     .... X... .... X...   (soft, slightly late)
Hat       x.g. x.g. x.g. x.gx   (loose, unquantised feel)
```
Processing: low-pass (~3 kHz), bitcrush, vinyl crackle, tape wow/flutter, sidechain. Arrangement: 2-3 min loops, elements in 4/8-bar phrases. References: Nujabes, J Dilla (influence), Lofi Girl channel artists.

**MODERN R&B** — 60-90 (or 120-140 in half-time trap-R&B). Keys: major 7 / minor 9 colours; progressions like IVmaj7–iii7–vi9, ii9–V13. Sounds: Rhodes, pads, plucked guitar, pitched vocal chops, airy reverbs.
```
Kick      X... .... ..x. ...g
Snare/Clp .... X... .... X...   (or beat-3 half-time snap in trap-R&B)
Hat       x.x. x.xx x.x. x.x.   (swung, ghost notes, occasional triplet)
Perc      finger snaps, rimshots, shakers
```
808 tuned to chord roots, smooth glides. References: The-Dream/Tricky Stewart (classic), modern: SZA, Brent Faiyaz, Summer Walker productions.

**AFROBEATS** — 95-115 (default 104-110). Keys: major (C, G, F, D) or minor; simple triads, I–V–vi–IV type loops. Sounds: muted guitar, marimba/kalimba plucks, piano, pads, log drum, talking drum.
```
Kick      X... ..x. x... ..x.   (1, 7, 9, 15 alt bars; vel 75-85 on 9)
Rim/Snare .... X... .... X..g   (2 & 4, ghost on step 14 every other bar)
Hat (cl)  x.g. x.g. x.g. x.g.   (8ths; 70-80 down / 50-60 up)
Open hat  .... ..x. .... ....   (choke group)
Shaker    xgxg xgxg xgxg xgxg   (16ths, 60-40-70-40%)
Clave 3-2 x.x. ..x. .... x.x.   (1, 3, 7, 13, 15)
Congas    syncopated fills, accents 90%, ghosts 35-50%
```
Swing: gentle 16th swing on shakers/percs. Bass: round, roots on 1, walking on 3. Arrangement: intro 4-8, verse 16, hook 8, bridge 8. References: Sarz, Kel-P, P2J; Burna Boy, Wizkid, Rema.

**AMAPIANO** — 108-115 (default 112-113). Keys: no fixed key; jazzy m9/maj7/6-9 chords, ii–V–I. Sounds: log drum, deep-house piano chords, pads, flutes/saxes, vocal chants, whistles.
```
Kick      X... X... X... X...   (4-floor or sparser syncopated in modern styles)
Clap/Rim  .... x... .... x...   (2 & 4, often a rim/snap)
Open hat  ..x. ..x. ..x. ..x.   (offbeats: 3, 7, 11, 15)
Shaker    xgxg xgxg xgxg xgxg   (constant 16ths, swung)
Conga     .... x..x ..x. x...   (2, "a" of 2, "and" of 3, 4 per Roland)
Log drum  ..x. .x.. x.x. ...x   (2-bar call/answer, between kicks; pitch-glide)
```
Log drum: Fruity DX10 preset or layered synth; soft clip/WaveShaper; tuned to key. Arrangement: long-form (intro 8-16, build 16, development 16-32, breakdown 8-16, outro 8-16). References: Kabza De Small, DJ Maphorisa, Vigro Deep, Kelvin Momo, De Mthuda.

**REGGAETON (DEMBOW)** — 85-100 (default 90-96); Dominican dembow 115-130. Keys: minor (Am, Dm, Em common), i–VI–III–VII loops. Sounds: plucks, synth leads, guitars, pads.
```
Kick      X... X... X... X...   (4-floor round kick; minimum 1 & 9)
Snare     ...x ..x. ...x ..x.   (steps 4, 7, 12, 15 = 3+3+2 dembow)
Hat       ..x. ..x. ..x. ..x.   (offbeat or 16ths)
Perc      timbales/congas fills, shakers
```
Bass: sustained sub/808 on roots, sidechained. References: DJ Luian & Mambo Kingz, Tainy; Bad Bunny, J Balvin; dembow: El Alfa.

**PHONK** — Memphis/classic: ~120-140 (often slower, lo-fi), drift: 130-160, Brazilian/mandelão: 130-150. Keys: minor/phrygian. Sounds: TR-808 cowbell melodies, Memphis vocal chops (pitched down), distorted 808, dark synths.
```
Drift:
Kick      X... ..x. X... ..x.
Snare/Clp .... X... .... X...   (or beat-3 half-time in Memphis style)
Hat       x.xx x.x. x.xx x.x.
Cowbell   melodic 16th line, syncopated, heavily distorted
Brazilian (tamborzão-derived):
Kick      X..x ..x. X..x ..x.
Snare/Perc ..x. ..x. ..x. ..x.
```
808: distorted, saturated, long. References: DJ Screw/Three 6 Mafia (roots), SpaceGhostPurrp, DJ Smokey, Kordhell, DVRST, Ghostface Playa.

**JERSEY CLUB** — 130-145 (default 140). Keys: minor/major chops. Sounds: chopped vocal hooks, bed squeak, sparse synth stabs.
```
Kick      X... X... X..X ..X.   (1, 5, 9, 12, 15)
Snare     .... .... x..x ....   (bars 1&3: 9, 12; bars 2&4: 9, 11, 12)
Hat       .... x... .... x...   (beats 2 and 4)
Bed squeak/vocal chop on 16ths after kicks
```
Arrangement: 8-bar blocks with stops/reverse tails. References: DJ Tameil, DJ Sliink, Uniique; Bandmanrill (Jersey drill).

**WEST COAST RATCHET / DETROIT** — ratchet ~95-105; Detroit ~165-190 (or half-time). Ratchet sounds: sparse 808 kicks, clap flurries, "hey" chants, simple synths; Detroit: piano loops, choirs, off-beat swung hats, drums.
```
Ratchet:
Kick      X... ..x. X... ..x.
Clap      .... X... .... X..x  (+ clap flurries)
Hat       x.x. x.x. x.x. x.x.
Detroit (felt in half-time):
Kick      X... .... X.x. ....
Snare     .... X... .... X...
Hat       x.x. x.x. x.x. x.x.  (off-beat accents)
```
References: Mustard, DJ Quik; Helluva, Sada Baby, Tee Grizzley.

## Q2. What tempo and swing do producers use, and how are drum patterns built step by step?

### Takeaway
Most hip hop/trap derivatives cluster at 130-150 BPM in half-time feel; global dance styles cluster around 95-115 (Afrobeats/amapiano) or 85-100 (reggaeton); boom bap and lo-fi rely on heavy swing (MPC 54-62% or more), while drill and trap rely on grid precision with hand-edited hat gaps and rolls. Step-by-step: lay the anchor (kick/snare skeleton), then hats/shakers, then rolls/fills and velocity shaping, then percussion accents.

### Cited Findings
- Trap: 130-200 BPM range noted; 140 typical with half-time feel; 32nd-note rolls with lower middle velocities — [Native Instruments trap](https://blog.native-instruments.com/how-to-make-a-trap-beat/)
- Drill: 140-150 BPM UK/NY; build snare first (bar 1 beat 3, bar 2 beat 4), hats from 8ths with gaps and bursts, kicks as pickups — [beatstorapon drill](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/how-to-make-a-drill-beat/)
- UK drill 138-151 BPM, 3+3+2 hats — [Wikipedia UK drill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_drill)
- Boom bap: MPC swing 54-62% typical; Attack used MPC 3000 8th 57% on kick/snare, 16th 74% on hats; nudged claps — [violetrecording boom bap](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-make-boom-bap-beats/) (via search summary); [Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/90s-boom-bap-hip-hop/)
- SP-1200/MPC swing 58-70% on 1/8 or 1/16 cited as producing the boom bap feel — [The Drum Broker blog](https://hiphopdrumsamples.com/blogs/the-drum-broker-blog/tagged/Akai%20MPC) (via search summary)
- Lo-fi 60-90 BPM, unquantised "janky" timing intentional — [Native Instruments lo-fi](https://blog.native-instruments.com/lo-fi-hip-hop/)
- Afrobeats 95-115 BPM, swung 16th shakers, explicit velocity percentages — [audeobox Afrobeats](https://www.audeobox.com/learn/fl-studio/how-to-make-afrobeats-in-fl-studio/)
- Amapiano 108-115 BPM, swung 16th shakers — [Roland](https://articles.roland.com/?p=67237); [Splice](https://splice.com/blog/what-is-amapiano-music/)
- Reggaeton 85-100; Dominican dembow 115-130 — [orphiq dembow](https://orphiq.com/resources/what-is-dembow)
- Jersey club 130-140 (NI example 142) — [Native Instruments Jersey club](https://blog.native-instruments.com/jersey-club/)
- Plugg 130-150 half-time — [Reason Studios plugg](https://www.reasonstudios.com/news/post/how-to-make-plugg-beats) (via search summary)
- Rage 140-170 — [musicproductionwiki](https://musicproductionwiki.com/articles/how-to-make-rage-beats) (low authority)
- Phonk: drift 130-160, Brazilian 130-150; mandelão 130/140 — [tunee.ai](https://www.tunee.ai/music-generator/phonk); [ABCdoABC](https://abcdoabc.com.br/brazilian-phonk-por-que-o-funk-brasileiro-ganhou-outro-nome-e-se-espalhou-pela-europa/)

### Inferences
- FL Studio mapping: MPC swing 50% = straight; MPC 57% roughly corresponds to light-moderate FL channel-rack swing (~20-30%); 62%+ is heavy (~40-50%). This mapping is approximate, not sourced.
- Step order for the skill: (1) set tempo; (2) program kick/snare skeleton; (3) hats/shakers; (4) rolls/ghosts/velocity; (5) percussion; (6) 808/bass following kick; (7) melody/chords; (8) arrangement.

### Gaps
- No source gave a calibrated FL Studio swing % equivalent per genre; the lo-fi "10-15% swing" figure appeared in a search snippet without verifiable attribution.

## Q3. What harmonic/melodic choices are typical?

### Takeaway
Dark minor modes (harmonic minor, phrygian b2) and short motifs dominate trap/drill/phonk; jazz extensions (m7, maj7, 9ths, 6/9) dominate lo-fi, R&B, pluggnb and amapiano; Afrobeats/reggaeton favour simple diatonic triad loops.

### Cited Findings
- Drill: minor key/mode, flattened 2nd for a colder colour; 2-4 note motifs over full progressions; one main sound + one support layer (low string/pad) — [beatstorapon drill](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/how-to-make-a-drill-beat/)
- Trap: C minor example, counter-melody from root/octave/5th/m7 — [NI trap](https://blog.native-instruments.com/how-to-make-a-trap-beat/)
- Pluggnb: block-chord electric piano, m7 and add9, close inversions — [Splice pluggnb](https://splice.com/blog/what-is-pluggnb/)
- Lo-fi: VI–i–V7 (F–Am–E7) with tensions — [NI lo-fi](https://blog.native-instruments.com/lo-fi-hip-hop/)
- Amapiano: Cm9–F7–Bbmaj9–Gm7–G7b9 — [Roland](https://articles.roland.com/?p=67237); Em9–Cmaj7–Gmaj7–D6/9 — [beatstorapon amapiano](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/how-to-make-amapiano-beat/)
- Afrobeats: simple triads in C/G/F/D major — [audeobox](https://www.audeobox.com/learn/fl-studio/how-to-make-afrobeats-in-fl-studio/)
- R&B: 7th/9th/11th/13th harmony, 808 tuned to chord roots — [violetrecording R&B](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-make-rnb-beats/)
- Reggaeton: bass follows chord roots — [orphiq reggaeton](https://orphiq.com/resources/what-is-reggaeton)
- Phonk: cowbell melodies, distorted bass, chopped Memphis vocals — [Wikipedia Phonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonk)

### Inferences
- For the skill: default scales — trap/drill/phonk: natural/harmonic minor, phrygian; rage: natural minor; plugg: major/minor pentatonic; pluggnb/R&B/lo-fi: dorian/major with 7th-9th chords; Afrobeats/reggaeton: major/minor diatonic triads; amapiano: dorian/minor with 9ths.

### Gaps
- No source quantified most common keys per genre.

## Q4. What are the common arrangement layouts?

### Takeaway
Rap-led styles use 8-bar intro/hook and 16-bar verses; dance/global styles use 8-bar block changes and, for amapiano, longer 16-32 bar developments.

### Cited Findings
- Drill template: intro 1-8, hook 9-16, verse 17-32, hook 33-40, verse 41-56, bridge 57-64, hook 65-72, outro 73-80; make a noticeable change every 4 or 8 bars — [beatstorapon drill](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/how-to-make-a-drill-beat/)
- Afrobeats: intro 4-8, verse 16, hook 8, bridge 8 — [audeobox](https://www.audeobox.com/learn/fl-studio/how-to-make-afrobeats-in-fl-studio/)
- Amapiano: intro 8-16, foundation 16, development 16-32, breakdown 8-16, outro 8-16 — [beatstorapon amapiano](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/how-to-make-amapiano-beat/)
- Jersey club: layer in every 8 bars, drop at bar 41, return at 49 — [NI Jersey club](https://blog.native-instruments.com/jersey-club/)
- Lo-fi: elements introduced in 4- or 8-bar phrases with reverse crashes — [NI lo-fi](https://blog.native-instruments.com/lo-fi-hip-hop/)

### Inferences
- Default rap template for the skill (trap/plugg/rage/boom bap/R&B): 8-bar intro, 8-bar hook, 16-bar verse, 8-bar hook, 16-bar verse, 8-bar hook, 4-8 bar outro; drop drums/808 for 1 bar before hooks.
